## The Skilled Migrant Category was rewritten again on 24 August 2026

New Zealand moved to a **six-point system** in October 2023, and then materially rewrote how the
points are earned with effect from **24 August 2026**:

- **New Zealand qualifications now score one point more** than the overseas equivalent
- **Level 8 and 9 points now require a bachelor's degree**
- The **Trades** experience requirement was reduced
- The wage threshold is now tested **as at the date your experience began**, not the date you apply
- English-test validity extended to **five years** for registered occupations

The **SMC wage threshold is NZ$35.00 an hour** from 9 March 2026, so the points multiples work out at
**NZ$52.50, NZ$70.00 and NZ$105.00** an hour.

## The Red and Amber lists now gate the skilled work experience pathway

This is new and it disqualifies people who would previously have been fine:

- **Amber list** occupations need **1.2× the wage threshold plus three years of New Zealand
  experience**
- **Red list** occupations are **not eligible for this pathway** — including beauty therapists,
  hairdressers and retail managers

If your occupation is on either list, the arithmetic changes completely. Check both lists before you
apply.

## Green List additions
Ten trades occupations were added to the **Work to Residence** pathway on **18 August 2025** —
welders, fitters, metal fabricators, panel beaters, vehicle painters and paving plant operators — at
NZ$43.63 or NZ$38.59 an hour, each requiring **24 months of New Zealand experience**.

## Straight to Residence: fee and timing

Immigration New Zealand lists the Straight to Residence Visa fee from **NZ$6,450** and reports 80% of
applications decided within 3.5 months. You must be 55 or younger when you apply, and no prior New
Zealand work is required.

## New Zealand citizenship and your Indian passport

New Zealand **does** permit dual citizenship. **India does not.** So the constraint here is entirely
on the Indian side.

New Zealand citizenship becomes possible after **1,350 days over five years**, with at least 240 days
in each of those years. Taking it would end your Indian citizenship automatically under Section 9(1)
of the Citizenship Act 1955, regardless of New Zealand's own generosity on the point.

Residence and permanent residence carry no such consequence. Most Indian families stop at permanent
residence for exactly that reason, and there is nothing wrong with doing so.
