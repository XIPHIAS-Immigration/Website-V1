## Overview

Italy's **Digital Nomad Visa** is a residence route for highly qualified professionals who work remotely using technology, either as **digital nomads** working for themselves or as **remote workers** employed by a company, which may be based outside Italy. It was introduced by the **Ministry of the Interior decree of 29 February 2024**, published in the *Gazzetta Ufficiale* (Serie Generale n. 79) on 4 April 2024 and applying from 5 April 2024.

The residence permit is issued for **one year** and is **renewable annually**, each time on the same conditions.

## Who qualifies

- **Non-EU nationality.**
- **Highly qualified work carried out remotely**, as a self-employed professional or as an employee. The decree allows work for a company *anche non residente*, that is, including one not resident in Italy. It does not require a foreign employer, and it sets no percentage cap on Italian work.
- **At least six months' prior experience** in the remote activity you will carry on from Italy.
- **Annual income of at least three times the minimum level for exemption from healthcare cost-sharing** (*esenzione dalla partecipazione alla spesa sanitaria*).
- **Health insurance** covering Italy and **suitable accommodation**.

The decree sets the income test as a formula rather than a fixed sum, and Italian consulates publish their own derived figures, which differ. The consulates in New York, Boston, Los Angeles and San Francisco, for example, state **€24,789** a year (as of 2024). XIPHIAS confirms the figure applied by the consulate handling your file before filing.

| Revenue stamp (*marca da bollo*) | €16 | Paid with the residence permit application |

The residence permit application also carries a permit contribution and the Poste Italiane kit charge. XIPHIAS confirms the contribution that applies to your permit before filing.

## Timeline

Consular processing times vary by consulate. After arrival you must apply for the residence permit (*permesso di soggiorno*) within **eight working days**. The permit then runs for one year and is renewed annually.

## Family

Your spouse and dependent children can join you under Italy's family reunification rules. XIPHIAS confirms the documents the consulate requires for each family member.

## Long-term residence and citizenship

Italian law grants the EU long-term residence permit after **five years** of holding a valid residence permit (article 9 of Legislative Decree 286/1998). The categories that article excludes (study, temporary protection, medical care and certain humanitarian permits) do not include a work-based permit of this kind, so on the face of the statute the years count. No Ministry circular yet confirms this in terms, so XIPHIAS treats it as the expected position rather than a guarantee.

Because the permit is issued one year at a time, reaching five years takes successive renewals, each re-testing income, accommodation and insurance.

Italian **citizenship** by residence requires **ten years** of legal residence. India does not permit dual citizenship: acquiring Italian citizenship would end Indian citizenship under Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955. Italian residence does not.

## Tax

The visa carries no tax concession of its own. Italy's **impatriate regime**, as reformed from 2024, excludes 50% of qualifying income from tax on income up to €600,000, for five tax periods. It is a separate regime with its own conditions, including a high level of qualification or specialisation that many remote workers will not meet, and it is forfeited, with interest, if you do not remain tax resident in Italy for four years. Take Italian tax advice before relying on it.

## Risks to plan for

- The permit runs one year at a time. A renewal that fails on income, accommodation or insurance ends the route.
- The income figure follows the healthcare-exemption level and differs between consulates.
- The eight-working-day deadline for the residence permit application runs from arrival.
- Counting these years towards long-term residence is the expected reading of the statute, not yet confirmed by Ministry guidance.
