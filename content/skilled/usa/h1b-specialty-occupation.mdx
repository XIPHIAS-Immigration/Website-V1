### Overview
The **H-1B visa** is a U.S. non-immigrant work visa that allows **employers to hire foreign professionals** in **specialty occupations** requiring **highly specialized knowledge** and at least a **bachelor’s degree or equivalent**. It’s one of the most common pathways for skilled professionals to work legally in the United States.

### Eligibility Overview
To qualify, the applicant must have a **valid job offer** from a **U.S. employer** in a specialty field such as IT, engineering, finance, healthcare, or science.  
The employer must file a **Labor Condition Application (LCA)** with the Department of Labor and submit **Form I-129 (Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker)** to USCIS.  
The H-1B is typically granted for **three years**, extendable to **six years**, and allows **dual intent** for future Green Card applications.

## What changed, and what it means if you are applying from India

The H-1B is still a real visa. But between September 2025 and September 2026 three separate changes
landed, and together they reshape who can realistically use it.

### 1. The USD 100,000 payment
Presidential Proclamation 10973, signed **19 September 2025** and effective **21 September 2025**,
requires a **USD 100,000 payment per new H-1B petition** where the beneficiary is **outside the
United States and must seek admission**. Employers must obtain and retain proof of payment before
filing. Proclamation 11069, signed **18 September 2026**, extended it to **21 September 2027**.

It is **one-time, not annual**. It does **not** apply to existing H-1B holders travelling, to
renewals, or to petitions filed before 21 September 2025. The Secretary of Homeland Security may
waive it for an individual, a company or a whole industry on national-interest grounds.

What this means in practice: if you are in India and an employer wants to sponsor you, the first
question is no longer whether you will be selected. It is whether your employer will pay
USD 100,000. Get that in writing before you spend anything on this route.

### 2. Selection now favours higher salaries
Since **27 February 2026** the cap registration is weighted by wage level. A registration goes into
the pool **once at Level I, twice at Level II, three times at Level III and four times at Level IV**.

That is a structural change, not a tweak. An entry-level candidate at Level I now carries roughly a
quarter of the selection weight of a Level IV role. For Indian IT candidates at the junior end, the
odds got materially worse.

### 3. Layoffs are now an adjudication factor
Executive Order 14431, signed **18 September 2026**, requires the State Department, Department of
Labor and DHS to consider whether your sponsoring employer has made layoffs in the previous year or
plans them. It also directed the Department of Labor to begin reviewing historical Labor Condition
Applications for enforcement.

So your employer's own history now affects your petition.

## The FY2027 cap is closed

Registration ran in **March 2026** and selection is complete. The next window is **FY2028, expected
around March 2027**. Anyone offering to register you for FY2027 now is not telling you the truth.

## What is coming

DHS proposed a **further USD 103,265 fee** on cap-subject petitions on **25 August 2026**. It is a
**proposed rule and is not in force** — but it is written to stack on top of the USD 100,000
payment, not replace it.

Separately, the Department of Labor proposed raising prevailing wage percentiles from 17/34/50/67 to
**34/52/70/88** on 27 March 2026. No final rule has been published. If it lands, it raises the
salary floor for H-1B and for EB-2 and EB-3 labour certification.

## The honest alternative

For a strong candidate, **[O-1A](/corporate/usa/o1-entrepreneur-visa)** is now often the better
route: no cap, no lottery, no weighted selection, no USD 100,000 payment, and no per-country queue.
It demands a genuine record of achievement, and we will tell you plainly whether yours reaches it.

**[L-1](/corporate/usa/l1-corporate-transfer-visa)** works if you already have a year with a
multinational's overseas arm. And if the goal is a green card rather than a US job,
**[EB-5 set-aside investment](/residency/usa/eb5-targeted-employment-area)** is the only
employment-based route with **no India queue at all** as at October 2026.
