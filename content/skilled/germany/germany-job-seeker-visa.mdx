## Overview

Germany's **Opportunity Card** (*Chancenkarte*) gives a qualified professional from outside the EU up to **one year** in Germany to find skilled work, and allows part-time work while the search is under way. It is governed by **sections 20a and 20b of the Residence Act** (*Aufenthaltsgesetz*) and has applied since **1 June 2024**, when it replaced the previous job seeker visa. Section 20 of the Act now covers job search only for people who have completed studies, research, vocational training or the recognition of a qualification in Germany.

For a professional based in India or the UAE who does not yet hold a German job offer, the Opportunity Card is the route into the German job market.

## Two ways to qualify

| Route | What you need |
|---|---|
| **Skilled-worker route** | A vocational or academic qualification fully recognised in Germany. No points and no language certificate are required. |
| **Points route** | A foreign degree, a state-recognised vocational qualification of at least two years, or a certificate from a German Chamber of Commerce Abroad (AHK), plus German at **A1** or English at **B2**, plus at least **6 points** |

## The points table

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Qualification partially recognised as equivalent in Germany | 4 |
| Occupation in a German shortage field | 1 |
| At least two years' professional experience in the last five years | 2 |
| At least five years' professional experience in the last seven years | 3 |
| German at A2 / B1 / B2 | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| English at C1 or native level | 1 |
| Aged under 35 / aged 35 to 40 | 2 / 1 |
| At least six months' previous legal residence in Germany | 1 |
| Spouse or partner also meets the Opportunity Card conditions and applies with you | 1 |

Experience earns points; it is not a condition. Neither route sets a minimum period of professional experience: five years in the last seven is worth three points on the scale.

## What the card allows

- **Up to one year** in Germany to look for skilled work, with no job offer needed to apply.
- **Part-time work of up to 20 hours a week** while you search.
- **Trial employment of up to two weeks** with each prospective employer.
- A move to an **EU Blue Card** or another work-based residence permit once you hold a qualifying offer. Where no other permit fits, the card can be extended as a follow-up card (*Folge-Chancenkarte*) for up to **two further years** with a qualified job offer.

## Costs and proof of funds

| Item | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| National (D) visa fee | €75 | INR 8,400 at German missions in India, plus the visa service provider's charge |
| Proof of funds, 2026 | €1,091 a month | Usually shown through a blocked account |
| Blocked account for a 12-month card, 2026 | €13,092 | Funds held in your name to cover living costs during the search |

The monthly amount is set for each year. Applicants also need health insurance covering the stay.

## Timeline

The Federal Foreign Office publishes no fixed processing time for national visas and states that long-stay visas may take several months. ## Family

The Opportunity Card is issued to the individual. A spouse or partner who also meets its conditions can apply for a card of their own, and applying together adds a point to your score. Family reunification is planned around the residence permit you move into once employed; XIPHIAS sets out the options for your family before you apply.

## The next step: EU Blue Card

Most Opportunity Card holders aim for an **EU Blue Card**. The 2026 salary thresholds are:

| Category | Gross annual salary, 2026 |
|---|---|
| General threshold | €50,700 |
| Shortage occupations, and graduates within three years of their degree | €45,934.20 |

IT specialists without a degree can qualify at the reduced threshold with three years of comparable experience in the last seven. Under the recognition partnership (*Anerkennungspartnerschaft*) introduced in March 2024, the recognition of a foreign qualification no longer has to begin before you enter Germany.

## Settlement and citizenship

Time on the Opportunity Card is a search period. Permanent residence and naturalisation are built on the residence permit you hold once employed, and XIPHIAS maps that timeline for the permit you actually obtain. India does not permit dual citizenship: acquiring German citizenship would end Indian citizenship under Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955. German residence does not.

## Risks to plan for

- The card lasts **at most one year**. Without qualifying work it cannot simply be renewed: the follow-up card requires a qualified job offer.
- Work is capped at **20 hours a week** plus two-week trials. Full-time employment needs a work-based residence permit.
- Every point must be documented. A partial equivalence, language level or period of experience that cannot be evidenced is not counted.
- Funds are assessed at the rate for the year of application: €1,091 a month in 2026.
