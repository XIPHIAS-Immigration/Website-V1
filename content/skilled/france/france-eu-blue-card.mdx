### Overview

The **EU Blue Card** — *carte de séjour "talent — carte bleue européenne"* — is France's permit for senior salaried professionals. It asks for a contract of at least **six months** paying at least **€59,373 gross per year**, plus either a **diploma covering three years of higher education** or **five years of professional experience** at a comparable level.

### What the card is worth

| | France EU Blue Card |
|---|---|
| **Salary floor** | €59,373 gross per year |
| **Qualification** | 3-year degree, or 5 years' comparable experience |
| **Minimum contract** | 6 months |
| **Card length** | Contract length, capped at 4 years |
| **Short contract** | Contract + 3 months, capped at 2 years |
| **Family** | Talent-famille, no reunification procedure |
| **EU mobility** | After 1 year on a Blue Card elsewhere |
| **French required** | Not for the card; B2 at naturalisation |

### The threshold is the real test

At €59,373 this is a senior permit, and there is no younger-applicant discount of the kind some EU states operate. If an offer sits below the floor, the Blue Card is closed and the question becomes whether another talent route fits — the innovative-company salaried route, for instance, sets its floor at **€39,582** where the employer is a *jeune entreprise innovante* or a company the economy ministry has recognised as innovative.

That is the sort of substitution worth checking before an offer is negotiated rather than after it is signed.
