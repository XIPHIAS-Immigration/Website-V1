  Dubai is the UAE's leading hub for company formation. A company can be set up in a free zone, licensed by the free zone authority, or on the mainland, licensed by the Department of Economy and Tourism. Either way, once the company holds an establishment card it can sponsor residence permits for its owners, employees and their families.
  Costs fall into three layers: small, published government immigration fees; charges set by each free zone or by DET; and professional fees. XIPHIAS sets out each layer separately, and advises on corporate tax and on the Indian tax position before you move.

faq:
  - q: "What does it cost to start a company?"
    a: "There is no single price. Government immigration fees are small: a GDRFA residence permit is AED 200 and a status change AED 500. Free zone charges vary by authority; DMCC publishes AED 1,015 for the application, AED 9,000 for registration and AED 2,020 for the articles of association, then AED 20,265 a year for a standard licence. Mainland DET fees are assessed per activity. The Golden Visa requires an investment of AED 2,000,000."
  - q: "How long is a UAE residence visa valid?"
    a: "Two years for sponsored employees, five years for Green Residence investors (AED 1,000,000) and ten years for the Golden Visa (AED 2,000,000), all renewable."
  - q: "Can I sponsor family members?"
    a: "Yes. The sponsor needs a salary of AED 4,000, or AED 3,000 plus accommodation. This covers a spouse, unmarried daughters, sons under 25 and children with special needs."
  - q: "Is a free zone company tax-free?"
    a: "Not automatically. Corporate tax is 0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9% above. A Qualifying Free Zone Person pays 0% only on Qualifying Income and must meet substance, transfer-pricing and de minimis conditions."
  - q: "How long does setup take?"
    a: "Licensing time depends on the free zone or on DET. Once licensed, ICP issues an establishment card in 2 days and GDRFA processes residence permits in 48 hours."
  - q: "Does UAE residence end Indian tax residence?"
    a: "Not by itself. Indian tax residence is decided under Section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on days spent in India and the deemed-residence rule. Take Indian tax advice before relocating."
