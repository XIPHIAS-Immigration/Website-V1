## Overview

"Self-sponsorship" is a widely used name for a structure, not a Home Office visa. The Home Office does not use the term. It describes two separate things: a **UK company that holds a sponsor licence**, and a **Skilled Worker** visa for a person that company employs, where that person also owns or directs the company.

The Home Office does not prohibit an owner-director being sponsored by their company. It does examine the arrangement closely, and the requirements have risen. Since 22 July 2025 a sponsored role must be at **degree level (RQF 6)** and pay at least **£41,700 a year or the going rate for the job, whichever is higher**. Since 8 January 2026 the worker needs English at **B2**.

This page sets out how the structure works, what it costs in Home Office fees, how the Home Office tests it, and what makes a case viable.

## How the structure works

1. **The company.** A UK private limited company is incorporated at Companies House and runs a real business.
2. **The sponsor licence.** The company applies to the Home Office for a Skilled Worker sponsor licence.
3. **The Certificate of Sponsorship.** Once licensed, the company assigns a Certificate of Sponsorship for a genuine, degree-level role.
4. **The visa.** The individual applies for a Skilled Worker visa using that certificate.
5. **Sponsor duties.** The company keeps its licence obligations for as long as it sponsors anyone.

Each step is decided on its own merits. A licence can be refused, and a visa can be refused even where a licence has been granted.

## What the role must meet

- **Skill level:** RQF 6 (degree level) or above. The minimum was raised from RQF 3 on 22 July 2025, and around 180 occupations lost eligibility.
- **Salary:** at least **£41,700** a year, or the going rate for the occupation if that is higher.
- **English:** CEFR level **B2** at the visa stage, since 8 January 2026.
- **Shortage occupations:** the Immigration Salary List is being replaced by a Temporary Shortage List of about 50 medium-skilled occupations, most of them expiring at the end of 2026. New applicants in those occupations cannot bring dependants.

## How the Home Office tests a company that sponsors its owner

The Home Office's published guidance addresses this structure directly.

- **Sponsor guidance Part 1, paragraph L8.8.** The Home Office considers whether "your organisation has been established, or exists, mainly to facilitate the entry or residence of a person who would not otherwise have permission to work in the UK."
- **Paragraph L8.9.** Its worked example is a foreign national who "registers a business with Companies House while resident outside the UK", where "it is considered unlikely that the company would otherwise exist if it were not for the foreign national's wish to enter the UK."
- **Paragraph SK3.7.** "If you assign a CoS for a role which is not genuine, we will refuse the worker's application and we will revoke your licence."
- **The genuine-vacancy test in the High Court.** In *R (Prestige Social Care Services Ltd) v Secretary of State for the Home Department* [2025] EWHC 2860 (Admin), decided on 2 December 2025, the court confirmed that a vacancy can be found not genuine **without any finding of dishonesty**. It is enough that the role is not "appropriate to the business in light of its business model, business plan and scale."

In practice, a newly incorporated company with no trading history, no other employees and a single director-shareholder has real difficulty showing that a degree-level role paying £41,700 or more is proportionate to its scale. It can fail that test without any suggestion of dishonesty, and the consequence is both a refused visa and a revoked licence.

## What makes a case viable

The cases that hold up share one feature: the business would exist, and would need the role, without the visa. In practice that means:

- **Real trading activity:** customers, contracts, revenue or committed funding, in place before the licence application.
- **A role that fits the business:** a degree-level job that the business model and plan genuinely require, at a salary the business can sustain from its revenue or funding.
- **Scale behind the role:** operations, a plan and finances that make a £41,700-plus salary proportionate.
- **A real business behind the UK company:** for example an established Indian or UAE business serving UK customers through its UK company.
- **Compliance from day one:** payroll, right-to-work checks, HR records and reporting that meet sponsor duties.

## Costs: official Home Office fees

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sponsor licence, small or charitable sponsor | £611 |
| Sponsor licence, medium or large sponsor | £1,682 |
| Certificate of Sponsorship | £525 per worker |
| Immigration Skills Charge, small or charitable sponsor | £480 for the first 12 months, then £240 per additional six months |
| Immigration Skills Charge, medium or large sponsor | £1,320 for the first 12 months, then £660 per additional six months |
| Visa fee, applying from outside the UK | £819 for up to three years; £1,618 for more than three years |
| Visa fee, applying inside the UK | £943 for up to three years; £1,865 for more than three years |
| Immigration Health Surcharge | £1,035 per adult per year; £776 per child under 18 per year |

The sponsor licence, Certificate of Sponsorship and Immigration Skills Charge are charged to the sponsoring company. Over five years the Immigration Skills Charge comes to **£2,400** for a small sponsor and **£6,600** for a medium or large one.

**Worked example.** A small company sponsoring one worker for five years, with the worker applying from outside the UK, pays the Home Office: sponsor licence £611, Certificate of Sponsorship £525, Immigration Skills Charge £2,400, visa fee £1,618 and Immigration Health Surcharge £5,175 (five years at £1,035), a total of **£10,329** before the worker's salary.

Company formation, accountancy, legal and XIPHIAS professional fees are separate and are not Home Office fees.

## Timeline

The structure runs in two stages, each decided by the Home Office: the sponsor licence first, then the visa. Before either, allow time to build the trading evidence that makes the case viable; this is often the longest part. ## Family

On a degree-level role, the worker's partner and children can join as dependants. Each pays their own visa fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge of £1,035 per adult and £776 per child for each year of the visa.

## Settlement

Under the current rules, the Skilled Worker route leads to **indefinite leave to remain after five years**, provided the company keeps its licence and continues to sponsor the role throughout.

As at 1 October 2026, the earned-settlement proposal in the May 2025 immigration White Paper, which would lengthen the standard qualifying period to ten years, has not been legislated. The consultation closed on 12 February 2026, and Statement of Changes HC 259 (9 July 2026) made no change to settlement periods. Separately, from 26 March 2027 the Home Office will require English at B2 for settlement on certain routes (HC 1619). XIPHIAS confirms the position for your route before you apply.

## Risks

- **Refusal and revocation together.** A role found not genuine leads to refusal of the visa and revocation of the licence (SK3.7), which also affects anyone else the company sponsors.
- **No dishonesty needed.** A role can fail on proportionality to the business model, plan and scale alone (*Prestige Social Care*, 2025).
- **Salary commitment.** The company must pay at least £41,700, or the going rate if higher, for the whole period of sponsorship.
- **Rule changes.** Skill level, salary, English and fees all rose between July 2025 and April 2026. XIPHIAS checks every figure on the date we file.
