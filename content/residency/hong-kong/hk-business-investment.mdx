## How the HK$30 million actually splits

The New Capital Investment Entrant Scheme, relaunched on **1 March 2024**, is often quoted as a flat
HK$30 million. It is really two components:

| Component | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Permissible assets of your choosing | **HK$27 million** |
| CIES Investment Portfolio, held with the Hong Kong Investment Corporation | **HK$3 million** |

You must separately show **net assets of at least HK$30 million throughout the six months before you
apply** — the investment and the net-asset test are two different requirements, and applications fail
on the second more often than people expect.

## What counts towards the HK$27 million

| Permissible investment asset | Cap |
| --- | --- |
| Equities listed on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong, traded in HKD or RMB | No separate cap |
| Debt securities listed on the Stock Exchange, or issued or guaranteed by the HKSAR Government, the Exchange Fund, MTR Corporation, the Airport Authority or listed companies | No separate cap |
| Certificates of deposit in HKD or RMB with a maturity of at least 12 months | **HK$3 million** |
| Subordinated debt issued by authorised institutions | No separate cap |
| SFC-authorised funds, REITs, investment-linked assurance schemes and open-ended fund companies | No separate cap |
| Private open-ended fund companies and limited partnership funds | **HK$10 million** in aggregate |
| Real estate, residential and non-residential | **HK$15 million** in aggregate, of which residential at most **HK$10 million** |

From **1 November 2026**, financial assets count only if they are acquired through designated accounts.

## How the application runs

1. **Net Asset Assessment** with the New CIES Office at InvestHK, showing net assets of at least
   HK$30 million held throughout the preceding six months.
2. **Approval-in-principle** from the Immigration Department. You may then stay as a visitor for up
   to 180 days to complete the investment.
3. **Formal approval**, with permission to stay for up to 24 months, followed by extensions of up to
   three years each while the Portfolio Maintenance Requirements are met.
4. **Permanent residency** after seven years of continuous ordinary residence, with a declaration that
   Hong Kong is your place of permanent residence.

The New CIES Office does not publish a processing standard, so timelines vary by case. XIPHIAS confirms
the current position before filing.

## Why Hong Kong is different for an Indian passport holder

Of the main Asia-Pacific destinations, **Hong Kong is the only one where the normal outcome never
costs you your Indian citizenship.**

Hong Kong **permanent residency is not a citizenship**. After seven years of continuous ordinary
residence you become a permanent resident and get a Hong Kong permanent identity card — and you keep
your Indian passport, because nothing has been renounced. Section 9(1) of India's Citizenship Act
1955 only bites when you *voluntarily acquire another citizenship*, and HKPR is not one.

The only step that would cost your Indian citizenship is separately naturalising as a **Chinese**
national — a discretionary process that requires renouncing your foreign nationality anyway. Almost
nobody on an investment or talent route does that.

Compare that with **Singapore** (citizenship two years after PR) and **New Zealand** (citizenship
after 1,350 days over five years): both offer real citizenship, and taking it would end your Indian
citizenship. Hong Kong gives you the long-term status without forcing that decision.
