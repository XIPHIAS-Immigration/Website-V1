## Two corrections worth making before anything else

**Cyprus is not in Schengen.** It is an EU member state, which is not the same thing. A Cypriot
residence permit does not give you Schengen travel rights. Plenty of pages in this industry conflate
the two, and it is the single most common misunderstanding about Cyprus.

**There is no Cypriot citizenship by investment.** The Cyprus Investment Programme was terminated on
**1 November 2020**, by decision of the Council of Ministers dated 13 October 2020. What remains is
**permanent residence** by investment — a different product with a different outcome.

We are also going to be straight about a limitation on our own side. On 30 September 2026 we could
not reach any Cypriot government source to re-verify the current thresholds: gov.cy blocks automated
access, mip.gov.cy is serving an expired security certificate, and moi.gov.cy disallows it. The
figures on this page are our working figures. **We confirm them in writing with the Civil Registry
and Migration Department for every file before a client commits funds**, and we would rather tell you
that than present an unverified number as settled fact.

## One thing to settle before you start

These are **residency** programmes, not citizenship. That distinction matters more for an Indian
passport holder than for most, because **India does not permit dual citizenship**: under Section 9(1)
of the Citizenship Act 1955, voluntarily acquiring another citizenship ends your Indian citizenship
automatically.

Holding European **residency** does not affect your Indian citizenship at all. Only naturalising
would. So the residence permit is the safe part; the decision point comes years later, if and when
you choose to naturalise.
