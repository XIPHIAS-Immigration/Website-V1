It was introduced in 2007 and is sometimes called the passive income visa or the retirement visa, though neither name is quite right: it has no retirement-age requirement, and holders are free to work or run a business in Portugal once resident.

  What the D7 asks for is proof of regular income from sources that do not depend on you turning up to work — a pension, rent from property, dividends, interest, royalties or intellectual property income. ## Who the D7 suits

The D7 works for a retiree with a pension, a landlord with rental income, a shareholder living on dividends, or anyone whose money arrives without them needing to be at a desk. It is the quiet route into Portugal: no investment to place, no business plan to defend, no capital at risk.

What it does ask is that Portugal genuinely becomes home. The 16-month presence requirement in the first two years is real and is checked at renewal.

## The income test

The threshold is arithmetic, not judgement: twelve times the Portuguese national minimum wage for the main applicant, plus 50% of that for a second adult and 30% for each child (Portaria 1563/2007).

For 2026, with the minimum wage at €920 a month, that is €11,040 for the main applicant, €5,520 for a second adult and €3,312 for each child — €16,560 for a couple, and €19,872 for a couple with one child.

Two practical points. First, the figure moves every January with the minimum wage, so XIPHIAS confirms the threshold in force before filing and again before your AIMA appointment. Second, consular officers have discretion, and a file that clears the floor by a euro is a file that invites questions. We build applications with a margin.

## The citizenship timeline

Portugal changed its nationality law on **19 May 2026** (Lei Orgânica 1/2026). Naturalisation now
requires **seven years** of legal residence for nationals of EU member states and Portuguese-speaking
countries, and **ten years for everyone else — including Indian nationals**. It was five years for
everyone before that date. Applications already pending on 19 May 2026 keep the old five-year rule.

**Permanent residence is unaffected and remains five years** (Lei 23/2007, art. 80.º).

If Portuguese citizenship on a five-year horizon is the reason you are considering this, plan on ten
years instead, and weigh that before you commit.

Note also that India does not permit dual citizenship: acquiring Portuguese citizenship would end
your Indian citizenship under Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955. Portuguese **residency** does
not.
