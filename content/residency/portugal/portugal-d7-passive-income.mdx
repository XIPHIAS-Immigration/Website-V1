overview: |
  The D7 is Portugal's residence visa for people who can support themselves without taking a job in Portugal. It was introduced in 2007 and is sometimes called the passive income visa or the retirement visa, though neither name is quite right: it is open at any age, and holders are free to work or run a business in Portugal once resident.

  What the D7 asks for is proof of regular income from sources that do not depend on you turning up to work — a pension, rent from property, dividends, interest, royalties or intellectual property income. The amount is tied to the Portuguese national minimum wage, so it moves each January. For 2026 the minimum wage is €920 a month, which puts the floor for a single applicant at €11,040 a year.

  It is the least expensive route into Portuguese residence, and unlike the Golden Visa it carries a real presence requirement: you are expected to make Portugal your home, not merely to hold a card.

## Who the D7 suits

The D7 works for a retiree with a pension, a landlord with rental income, a shareholder living on dividends, or anyone whose money arrives without them needing to be at a desk. It is the quiet route into Portugal: no investment to place, no business plan to defend, no capital at risk.

What it does ask is that Portugal genuinely becomes home. The 16-month presence requirement in the first two years is real and is checked at renewal.

## The income test

The threshold is arithmetic, not judgement: twelve times the Portuguese national minimum wage for the main applicant, plus half of that for a second adult and just under a third for each child.

For 2026, with the minimum wage at €920 a month, that is €11,040 for one person, €16,560 for a couple, and €19,872 for a couple with one child.

Two practical points. First, the figure moves every January, and AIMA applies the rate in force when your appointment happens rather than when you filed — with current backlogs that can be a year apart. Second, consular officers have discretion, and a file that clears the floor by a euro is a file that invites questions. We build applications with a margin.
