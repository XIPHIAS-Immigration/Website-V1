## Two visas, one name

Almost everything written about the D8 skips the most consequential decision: which of the two versions you apply for.

The **temporary stay visa** runs for a year, allows multiple entries, and can be renewed. There is no residence permit to arrange after arrival. It suits someone who wants more than the 90 days Schengen allows but is not ready to commit.

The **long-stay visa** is a four-month entry document. You arrive, register with AIMA, and convert it into a two-year residence permit, renewable. Only this version builds the continuous lawful residence that permanent residence is counted from.

The income and document requirements are identical. The difference is entirely in what the time is worth afterwards.

## The number that moves

€3,680 a month, for 2026. It is four times the national minimum wage, so it moves every January — and it has moved a lot: the same requirement was €3,040 as recently as 2023.

The detail that catches people is timing. AIMA assesses against the threshold in force when your appointment takes place, not when you filed. With appointment backlogs running into months, a file built to last year's number can be short by the time it is read. We build to the threshold you will actually be assessed against.

## There is no savings requirement

You will see €11,040 and €44,160 quoted as a D8 “savings” or “bank balance” figure, sometimes on
very confident-looking pages. Neither is real.

The D8's legal basis is **Decreto Regulamentar n.º 4/2022**, which added articles 18.º-B and 31.º-A
to the 2007 regulation. Both list exactly four documents, and the only financial one is *proof of
average monthly income over the last three months of at least four times the guaranteed minimum
monthly wage*. No balance, no deposit, no lump sum.

Where the wrong numbers come from: **€11,040** is the **D7** rule (twelve times the minimum wage,
Portaria 1563/2007 art. 5.º n.º 6) borrowed by mistake. **€44,160** multiplies the D8's monthly
multiplier by the D7's twelve-month period and has no basis in any instrument.

What you actually need is **€3,680 a month**, evidenced across the preceding three months.

## The citizenship timeline, stated honestly

Portugal changed its nationality law on **19 May 2026** (Lei Orgânica 1/2026). Naturalisation now
requires **seven years** of legal residence for nationals of EU member states and Portuguese-speaking
countries, and **ten years for everyone else — including Indian nationals**. It was five years for
everyone before that date. Applications already pending on 19 May 2026 keep the old five-year rule.

**Permanent residence is unaffected and remains five years** (Lei 23/2007, art. 80.º).

A great many consultancy pages, and some of our own older articles, still say five years. They are
out of date. If Portuguese citizenship on a five-year horizon is the reason you are considering this,
that reason no longer exists — and you should know before you commit, not after.

Note also that India does not permit dual citizenship: acquiring Portuguese citizenship would end
your Indian citizenship under Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955. Portuguese **residency** does
not.
