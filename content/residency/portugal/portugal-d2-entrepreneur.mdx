## A visa decided on judgement, not a number

Most residence routes have a threshold. You meet it or you do not. The D2 does not work that way, and applicants who treat it as a cheaper Golden Visa tend to be refused.

What the consulate is deciding is whether your business will actually exist. That means a plan with a defensible market, costings that add up, funding visibly in place, and a founder whose background makes the venture plausible. A template plan for a consultancy with no clients is refused; a modest but concrete venture with signed agreements and money in a Portuguese account is not.

## What it costs to be credible

Nothing in Portuguese law states a figure. In practice, plans funded below roughly €50,000 attract scrutiny about whether the business can survive its first year, and that scrutiny is what sinks applications.

Separately from the business, you must show personal means of subsistence — €11,040 a year for the main applicant in 2026, with the usual uplifts of 50% for a spouse and 30% for each child. The business funding does not substitute for this.

## After approval

The initial permit runs two years and renews for three-year periods. You will deal with AIMA, not SEF — SEF was abolished in October 2023 and its residence functions moved across. Permanent residence becomes available after five years of lawful residence.

## The citizenship timeline, stated honestly

Portugal changed its nationality law on **19 May 2026** (Lei Orgânica 1/2026). Naturalisation now
requires **seven years** of legal residence for nationals of EU member states and Portuguese-speaking
countries, and **ten years for everyone else — including Indian nationals**. It was five years for
everyone before that date. Applications already pending on 19 May 2026 keep the old five-year rule.

**Permanent residence is unaffected and remains five years** (Lei 23/2007, art. 80.º).

A great many consultancy pages, and some of our own older articles, still say five years. They are
out of date. If Portuguese citizenship on a five-year horizon is the reason you are considering this,
that reason no longer exists — and you should know before you commit, not after.

Note also that India does not permit dual citizenship: acquiring Portuguese citizenship would end
your Indian citizenship under Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955. Portuguese **residency** does
not.
