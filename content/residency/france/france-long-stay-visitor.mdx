## Overview

The **visitor** card lets you live in France without an employer, a business or an investment. In exchange, it forbids **all work** — and the prefecture asks you to sign a handwritten declaration confirming you understand that.

Three conditions carry the application: **sufficient resources** for the whole stay, **health insurance** covering it, and the undertaking not to work. For a single person the resources floor is **€1,477.93 net per month**, evidenced across a year, which the official guidance also states as **€17,735.19** annually.

## The caveat nobody mentions

This route is often sold as a quiet path to French citizenship. Be careful with that.

Naturalisation requires five years of residence, and separately requires **stable and sufficient income from professional integration** — language the official guidance calls an essential condition of assimilation. A permit that forbids every form of work sits uncomfortably beside that requirement.

We could find no official statement that visitor years are excluded from the five years, and none that they count. That is exactly the kind of gap where a confident answer from a consultant should make you suspicious. If citizenship is your objective rather than a bonus, a talent card is the better foundation, and we would rather say so now than after you have spent a year on the wrong permit.
