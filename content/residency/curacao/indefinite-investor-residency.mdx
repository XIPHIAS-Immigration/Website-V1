## Read this before you plan around any Curaçao figure

Curaçao **enacted changes to its immigration law in May 2026**. XIPHIAS confirms the current
requirements under the 2026 legislation with the Immigratiedienst before filing.

The tiers below are the Wealthy Investor structure published by the Immigratiedienst for the scheme
introduced on 6 September 2014. They also sit in **policy rather than in the implementing decree** — the 1963 instrument contains no
investor provisions at all, which means they can be changed administratively without new legislation.

| Investment | Permit |
| --- | --- |
| XCG 500,000+ | 3 years |
| XCG 750,000+ | 5 years |
| XCG 1,500,000+ | Indefinite |

The investment must demonstrably benefit the local economy, and you must show permanent and
independently sufficient means of subsistence. Processing takes up to four months.

**We confirm current figures with the Immigratiedienst before any client commits funds.**

## What you would actually be acquiring

There is **no Curaçaoan citizenship**. Naturalisation through Curaçao yields **Netherlands
nationality** — governed Kingdom-wide — which carries **EU citizenship**. That is the real attraction,
and the reason Curaçao draws more investor interest than an island of its size would suggest.

For an Indian national it is also the catch: Dutch nationality is a foreign citizenship, so acquiring
it would end your Indian citizenship under Section 9(1). The residence permit itself does not.

## Your Indian passport is safe on a residency

Section 9(1) of India's Citizenship Act 1955 ends Indian citizenship only on the **voluntary
acquisition of another country's citizenship**. Holding a residence permit, permanent residence or a
long-term visa does none of that.

So every route on this page is safe to hold. What differs between countries is the **endpoint** — and
we will tell you plainly where a route can never become a citizenship, where it can, and what taking
that step would cost you.
