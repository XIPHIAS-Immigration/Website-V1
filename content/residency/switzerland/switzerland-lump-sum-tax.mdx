## Switzerland has no investment threshold — this is a tax route

Our pages previously showed **CHF 250,000** as a minimum investment. **No such requirement exists.**
Switzerland has no residency-by-investment programme. What people mean by "the Swiss route" is
**lump-sum taxation** — an agreement with a canton to be taxed on deemed expenditure rather than actual
income — and the residence permit that can follow from it.

## It is cantonal, and it has been abolished in five cantons

| Abolished | Still available |
| --- | --- |
| Zurich (from 1 Jan 2010), Schaffhausen, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, Basel-Landschaft, Basel-Stadt | Thurgau, St Gallen, Lucerne, Bern and others |

## The tax base moves, and it is not one number
The base is the **highest** of three things: the federal minimum, the canton's own minimum, or **seven
times your annual rent or imputed rental value**.

The federal minimum is **indexed** — for tax year 2025 it was **CHF 434,700**, not the CHF 400,000
figure from the 2016 statute that still circulates. Cantonal minima vary a great deal: **Schwyz sets
CHF 600,000**. Anyone quoting a single flat national figure has not read the cantonal tables.

## What the permit actually demands
- generally **age 55 or over**
- important ties to Switzerland
- a **formal undertaking not to engage in any gainful activity** in Switzerland or abroad, other than
  managing your own wealth
- a copy of the lump-sum tax agreement with the cantonal tax administration

**Federal SEM approval is mandatory** — the permit is a derogation from the ordinary admission rules,
and it is discretionary.

## Citizenship
Ten years, with a C permit and two to five years in the commune and canton. Switzerland has permitted
dual nationality without restriction since 1992 — so the only obstacle for an Indian client is the
Indian rule, not the Swiss one.

## Your Indian passport is safe on a residency

Section 9(1) of India's Citizenship Act 1955 ends Indian citizenship only on the **voluntary
acquisition of another country's citizenship**. Holding a residence permit, permanent residence or a
long-term visa does none of that.

So every route on this page is safe to hold. What differs between countries is the **endpoint** — and
we will tell you plainly where a route can never become a citizenship, where it can, and what taking
that step would cost you.
