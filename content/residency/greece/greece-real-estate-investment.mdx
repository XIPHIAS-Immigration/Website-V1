## The thresholds changed — €250,000 is no longer the headline

Law 5100/2024 replaced a single national figure with three:

| Where / what | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Attica, Thessaloniki regional unit, Mykonos, Thira, islands over 3,100 population | **€800,000** |
| Rest of Greece | **€400,000** |
| Change of use from commercial to residential, or restoration of a listed building | **€250,000** |

Two conditions that most pages leave out, and that decide real cases: the property must have a
minimum of **120 square metres of main spaces**, and the **single-property rule** applies — you must
reach the threshold with one property, not by assembling several. Short-term letting of the
qualifying property is **banned**.

Our own pages carried €250,000 as the headline until this review. That figure now applies only to
the two narrow cases in the last row.

## One thing to settle before you start

These are **residency** programmes, not citizenship. That distinction matters more for an Indian
passport holder than for most, because **India does not permit dual citizenship**: under Section 9(1)
of the Citizenship Act 1955, voluntarily acquiring another citizenship ends your Indian citizenship
automatically.

Holding European **residency** does not affect your Indian citizenship at all. Only naturalising
would. So the residence permit is the safe part; the decision point comes years later, if and when
you choose to naturalise.
