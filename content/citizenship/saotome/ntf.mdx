## Overview

São Tomé and Príncipe grants nationality for a **non-refundable contribution from USD 90,000**, under a
decree in force since August 2025. There is no residence requirement. It is one of the least expensive
citizenship programmes anywhere — and the passport is correspondingly one of the weaker ones, reaching
under 60 destinations, with **no Schengen and no UK access**.

This page had a number of serious errors in it. They are set out below rather than quietly corrected.

## The legal basis — and what we got wrong

The programme is real and it has a statute behind it. The chain is:

- **Lei n.º 07/2022 de 10 de Março**, the Nationality Law (*Diário da República*, I Série, n.º 25).
  **Article 10(2)** allows nationality for investment or for donation by way of direct financial
  support to the country, and **Article 10(3)** allows the residence requirement to be waived.
- **Decreto-Lei n.º 07/2025**, the implementing regulation — approved by the Council of Ministers on
  10 May 2025, promulgated 28 July 2025, published in the *Diário da República*, I Série, n.º 33, of
  **1 August 2025**, in force from publication.
- Applications opened on **1 September 2025**. The Citizenship Investment Unit published a first-year
  report on 1 September 2026.

The contribution goes to a **Fundo Nacional de Transformação** (National Transformation Fund). Note
that the programme's own channel describes the payment as a **National Development Contribution**,
so the naming is not consistent even at source; and we could not verify the fund's own constituting
instrument, governance or audited accounts. We say "contribution to the fund named in the decree"
rather than implying a fund whose charter we have read.

**Three corrections to what this page used to say.** It was tagged as a Caribbean programme — São
Tomé and Príncipe is an archipelago in the **Gulf of Guinea, off Central Africa**. It claimed 145
visa-free destinations, which is a Caribbean figure, not this one. And it carried a fee table and a
list of featured real estate projects that belong to entirely different programmes. All three have
been removed.

## Before anything else: what this means for an Indian passport

**India does not allow dual citizenship.** Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955 says that any
citizen of India who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country **shall cease to be a
citizen of India**.

Acquiring São Toméan citizenship is voluntary. So your Indian citizenship ends **automatically, by operation
of law, on the day you acquire it**. There is no Indian decision to appeal, no discretion to exercise
and no way to defer it. You must surrender your Indian passport and obtain a Surrender Certificate.

You can then apply for **Overseas Citizen of India** status. The Ministry of External Affairs is
explicit that *OCI is not to be misconstrued as dual citizenship* and confers no political rights. As
an OCI you cannot vote, cannot hold constitutional posts, do not have equality in public employment,
and **cannot acquire agricultural land, farmhouses or plantation property in India**.

This is a **replacement** of your Indian citizenship, not an addition to it. Any consultant who tells
you otherwise is either mistaken or not telling you the truth. São Toméan itself permits dual citizenship and requires no
renunciation — the restriction is entirely on the Indian side.

Our previous fee table listed application fees of USD 25,000 to 30,000, due diligence of USD 10,000

The page also advertised approved real estate at USD 325,000 and USD 600,000. We have found no

provides for is investment or donation.

## How long it actually takes

Not six weeks. The Citizenship Investment Unit's own first-year report, published 1 September 2026,
records:

- fastest approval: **26 days**
- **average approval: about two months**
- **average time to passport in hand: about four months**
- **29 passports issued against 548 applications** in the first year

That last figure is worth sitting with. This is a young programme working through a large backlog;
approval is not a formality and the passport does not follow immediately on approval.

There is **no residence requirement** — the decree waives it under Article 10(3), and there is no
minimum stay to keep the nationality.

## Who can be included — with two removals and one live caveat

- **Spouse**, including a recognised de facto partner. Confirmed.
- **Children.** Programme materials describe children under 30 who are financially dependent. But
  **Lei 07/2022 Article 10(4) contemplates only minor children**, and a Citizenship Investment Unit
  memorandum of **10 April 2026 placed passport issuance for adult dependent children aged 18 and
  over on hold**, pending a revised dependency law that has not been enacted. So for now we will not
  sell you an adult child's passport on this programme. We will tell you the position on the day you
  file.
- **Parents aged 55 and over**, financially dependent. Supported by programme materials.
- **Grandparents — removed.** No source supports their inclusion.
- **Siblings — removed.** No source supports their inclusion at any age.

## A bar that catches more people than you would expect

**Article 11(1) of Lei 07/2022 prohibits naturalisation for anyone already holding more than two
foreign nationalities.** And the Citizenship Investment Unit memorandum of 10 April 2026 put all new
applications from persons holding **three or more foreign nationalities on hold** pending a
parliamentary amendment that has not been finalised.

The programme's appointed agent channel currently states there is no limit on other nationalities.
That contradicts both the statute and the unit's own memorandum. We follow the statute.

## What the passport actually reaches

Around **58 to 61 destinations** visa-free or visa-on-arrival, depending on the index — the Henley
Passport Index puts it at 58 and ranks the passport around 74th for 2026; Arton's Passport Index
gives 61 visa-free plus one visa-on-arrival and ranks it around 80th. Rankings are commercial
indices, not government data, so we name the source and the year.

The **"145 countries" figure previously on this page was wrong by a factor of well over two.** It is
the Caribbean citizenship-programme number and it does not describe this passport. The "60+" that
appeared elsewhere on the same page was the accurate one.

Two things the passport does **not** give you, and they are the two most people ask about:

- **No Schengen access.** São Tomé and Príncipe is in **Annex I** of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 — the
  list of third countries whose nationals must hold a visa.
- **No UK access.** It sits at item 85 on the UK's visa national list.

What it does reach includes Singapore, Hong Kong, South Africa, Türkiye, the UAE, Qatar and most
Portuguese-speaking countries.

## On tax

We have removed the claim that São Tomé and Príncipe has no wealth or inheritance tax and very low
personal income tax. We could not confirm it from any official, IMF or World Bank source.

It is also the wrong question. **Citizenship does not make you tax resident anywhere.** Unless you
actually move to São Tomé and Príncipe, its domestic tax treatment has no bearing on what you pay.
Any consultant leading with a tax benefit on a citizenship product is selling you something that
does not follow.

## What we are watching

The statute is sound. The **concession is contested**: the programme is administered under a ten-year
exclusive concession, and the Tribunal de Contas (Court of Auditors) has identified irregularities in
that contract, including that it was signed before the enabling legislation was published. That is a
programme-continuity question, not a legality question about the nationality itself, but you should
know it exists before you file.

The programme is **not** on the FATF grey list or blacklist, and we found no EU, OECD or Transparency
International action naming it.

Before we take a file we obtain the gazette copy of Decreto-Lei n.º 07/2025 for your record, rather
than relying on a transcription.
