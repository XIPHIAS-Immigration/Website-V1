overview: |
  São Tomé and Príncipe — an archipelago in the Gulf of Guinea off Central Africa — grants nationality
  for investment or donation under Article 10(2) of Lei n.º 07/2022, implemented by Decreto-Lei n.º
  07/2025 (Diário da República I Série n.º 33, 1 August 2025). Applications opened on 1 September 2025.
  The minimum contribution is USD 90,000 for a single applicant, USD 95,000 for a family of two to four,
  plus a USD 5,000 non-refundable submission fee. There is no residence requirement. The unit's own
  first-year report puts average approval at about two months and average time to passport at about four.

faq:
  - q: "How long does the process take?"
    a: "The Citizenship Investment Unit's first-year report of 1 September 2026 records a fastest approval of 26 days, an average approval of about two months, and an average of about four months to passport in hand. It also records 29 passports issued against 548 applications. The six-week figure we previously published was a marketing target, not an outcome."
  - q: "Can family members be included in my application?"
    a: "Spouse, including a recognised de facto partner, and dependent children. Parents aged 55 and over who are financially dependent are provided for in programme materials. Grandparents and siblings are not — we have removed both. Passports for adult dependent children aged 18 and over are currently on hold pending a revised dependency law."
  - q: "Are there residency requirements?"
    a: "No. Article 10(3) of Lei 07/2022 allows the residence requirement to be waived, and there is no minimum stay to keep the nationality."
  - q: "Where can I travel visa-free?"
    a: "Around 58 to 61 destinations depending on the index, including Singapore, Hong Kong, South Africa, Türkiye, the UAE, Qatar and most Portuguese-speaking countries. There is no Schengen access — São Tomé and Príncipe is in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 — and no UK access."
  - q: "Can I hold dual citizenship?"
    a: "São Tomé and Príncipe permits it: Article 15(1) of Lei 07/2022 provides that a national who acquires another nationality keeps theirs unless they declare otherwise. But Article 11(1) bars naturalisation to anyone already holding more than two foreign nationalities. And India does not permit dual citizenship at all — an Indian citizen who acquires this nationality ceases to be a citizen of India."
  - q: "Are there tax benefits?"
    a: "We have removed that claim. We could not confirm it from any official, IMF or World Bank source, and citizenship does not make you tax resident anywhere in any event."
