## Overview
Approved developer real estate in St Kitts & Nevis qualifies at **USD 325,000**. Private real estate
is **USD 325,000** for a condominium or **USD 600,000** for a single-family house.

These minimums were **reduced** from USD 400,000 and USD 800,000 by SRO 43 of 2024 in October 2024 —
St Kitts moved in the opposite direction to the rest of the region. The holding period is **seven
years**, the longest of the five.
## Travel access, stated accurately

Passport-power league tables age badly and most consultancy pages quote them years out of date.
Here is where **St Kitts & Nevis** actually stands today:

**United Kingdom: visa-free, but you need an ETA.** Since 8 January 2025 an electronic travel authorisation is required before travel. “Visa-free” without that qualifier is no longer accurate.

**United States: a visa is required**, as it is for every Caribbean CBI passport. No programme in this region gives visa-free entry to the United States, whatever you may have been told.

**Canada: a visitor visa is required.** None of the five Caribbean citizenship-by-investment
countries has visa-free access to Canada.

**On Schengen and the EU:** access is currently in place, but the EU's revised visa suspension
mechanism, adopted on 31 December 2025, makes operating a citizenship-by-investment scheme a
self-standing ground for suspending visa-free travel. The European Commission wrote to Antigua &
Barbuda in June 2026 seeking a phase-out, and an EU report is due in December 2026. We will not tell
you Schengen access is permanent, because nobody can.

## What this does to an Indian passport

**India does not allow dual citizenship.** Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955 provides that a
citizen of India who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country **ceases to be a citizen
of India**. Acquiring any Caribbean citizenship is voluntary, so your Indian citizenship ends
**automatically on the day it is granted** — no Indian decision, no discretion, no deferral. The
Indian passport must be surrendered and a Surrender Certificate obtained.

You can then apply for **Overseas Citizen of India** status. The Ministry of External Affairs states
plainly that OCI is not dual citizenship: no vote, no constitutional posts, no equality in public
employment, and **no acquisition of agricultural land, farmhouses or plantation property in India**.

This is a **replacement** for your Indian citizenship, not an addition. Plan around that.
