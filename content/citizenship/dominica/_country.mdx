### What the table shows

**Grenada is the only one of the five that is simultaneously a US E-2 treaty country, free of any US
entry restriction, and still visa-free to the UK.** If the United States is your objective, that is
the deciding factor. Whether an individual qualifies for an E-2 visa is decided by US consular officers.

**Dominica and Saint Lucia have both lost UK visa-free access** — Dominica in July 2023, Saint Lucia
in March 2026, both expressly because of their citizenship programmes.

**Antigua and Dominica are both under US Presidential Proclamation 10998** since 1 January 2026,
which suspends immigrant entry and the B, F, M and J categories.

**No Caribbean citizenship-by-investment passport gives visa-free entry to the United States.**

## What this does to an Indian passport

**India does not allow dual citizenship.** Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955 provides that a
citizen of India who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country **ceases to be a citizen
of India**. Acquiring any Caribbean citizenship is voluntary, so your Indian citizenship ends
**automatically on the day it is granted** — no Indian decision, no discretion, no deferral. The
Indian passport must be surrendered and a Surrender Certificate obtained.

You can then apply for **Overseas Citizen of India** status. The Ministry of External Affairs states
plainly that OCI is not dual citizenship: no vote, no constitutional posts, no equality in public
employment, and **no acquisition of agricultural land, farmhouses or plantation property in India**.

This is a **replacement** for your Indian citizenship, not an addition. Plan around that.
