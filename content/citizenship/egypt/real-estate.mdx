## Overview

**USD 300,000 minimum** in Egyptian real estate, transferred from abroad. The amount was set by PM
Decree No. 876 of 2023.

## The legal basis

Egypt's investment route to citizenship sits on **Law No. 140 of 2019**, read with Investment Law
No. 72 of 2017, and is implemented by **Prime Ministerial Decree No. 3099 of 2019** as amended by
**PM Decree No. 876 of 2023** (Official Gazette issue 9 *bis*, 2 March 2023) and **PM Decree No. 3562
of 2023**. Decree 876/2023 is the one that produced today's amounts — it cut them substantially from
the earlier regime.

The programme is administered by the citizenship unit of the **Cabinet** (created by PM Decree No.
647 of 2020), not by the Ministry of Interior. If you are quoted different figures from the GAFI
investment-authority fact sheet, note that GAFI still publishes a **superseded pre-2023 sheet**; we
work from the Cabinet portal.

## The five-year rule

The official Arabic is explicit, and it matters:

> If the property is disposed of before five years have elapsed from the date of its acquisition,
> retaining Egyptian nationality is conditional on depositing **USD 250,000** at the Central Bank.

So selling early does **not** automatically void the citizenship — it triggers a further **USD 250,000
payment to keep it**. That is a materially different proposition from "you must hold for five years",
and it is the version you should plan around. The same rule applies to the investment-project route if
the project is liquidated or the stake transferred inside five years.

## Before anything else: what this means for an Indian passport

**India does not allow dual citizenship.** Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955 says that any
citizen of India who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country **shall cease to be a
citizen of India**.

Acquiring Egyptian citizenship is voluntary. So your Indian citizenship ends **automatically, by operation
of law, on the day you acquire it**. There is no Indian decision to appeal, no discretion to exercise
and no way to defer it. You must surrender your Indian passport and obtain a Surrender Certificate.

You can then apply for **Overseas Citizen of India** status. The Ministry of External Affairs is
explicit that *OCI is not to be misconstrued as dual citizenship* and confers no political rights. As
an OCI you cannot vote, cannot hold constitutional posts, do not have equality in public employment,
and **cannot acquire agricultural land, farmhouses or plantation property in India**.

This is a **replacement** of your Indian citizenship, not an addition to it. Egypt itself permits
dual citizenship and requires no renunciation — the restriction is entirely on the Indian side.

## Who can be included

- **Spouse — not automatic, and not immediate.** A spouse does not acquire Egyptian nationality as a
  dependant of the investor. Under Articles 6 and 7 of Law No. 26 of 1975 a foreign wife of an
  Egyptian acquires his nationality only if she notifies the Minister of Interior of her wish **and
  the marriage has subsisted for two years from that notification**. In practice the spouse's
  passport follows roughly two years later; the statute does not provide for it to be issued at the
  same time as the investor's.
- **Children.** Minor children of a naturalised person acquire the nationality with the parent, with
  a right to elect their original nationality within one year of reaching majority (Law 26/1975). The
  Cabinet's published application requirements refer only to "wives and children" and **state no age
  limit**. An "under 21" rule does not appear in the published instrument, so XIPHIAS
  confirms the age position on each file.
- **Parents, grandparents and siblings cannot be included.** There is no provision for them in any
  Egyptian instrument.

## How long it takes — the official standard

The Cabinet publishes a staged standard:

- Preliminary examination and decision: **three to six months** from submission.
- Then **six months** of temporary residence in Egypt to execute the investment.
- Final citizenship decision: **within a maximum of three months** after the investment is completed.
- The Cabinet's own FAQ states the overall timeframe as **six to twelve months**.

There is **no permanent residence requirement** to hold or keep the citizenship. But the process is
not fully remote: on preliminary approval you are granted a right of temporary residence in Egypt
for six months in order to execute the programme. Reports during 2025 also described new in-person
verification visits. No Egyptian government publication sets these out as a rule, so XIPHIAS
confirms the current position with the Cabinet unit before filing, and you should plan for some
travel to Egypt.

## Travel access — what the passport actually does

An Egyptian passport reaches roughly **50 destinations** visa-free or visa-on-arrival (Henley
Passport Index, 2026, which ranks it around 86th). Passport rankings are commercial indices, not
government data, and they change from year to year.

**Nigeria is not a visa-on-arrival destination for Egyptians**: it abolished visa-on-arrival
entirely on 1 May 2025 and replaced it with an eVisa.

Egypt has **no EU or UK visa waiver**, and this citizenship does not create one.

## Egypt and the US E-2 visa

Egypt is a **US E-2 treaty country, in force since 27 June 1992**. It is **E-2 only** — Egypt is not
an E-1 treaty trader country. India is not a treaty country at all, which is usually why Egypt is on
the table.

One qualification. On 14 January 2026 the US State Department announced a suspension of **immigrant
visa** issuance for nationals of 75 countries pending a public-charge reassessment, effective 21
January 2026, and Egypt is reported to be on that list. It does not touch nonimmigrant categories,
so E-2 is unaffected — but an Egyptian passport is currently a poor vehicle for a US **green card**
route. Egypt is **not** on Presidential Proclamation 10998's travel-ban list.

## What the citizenship does and does not give you

- **Political rights are deferred.** Article 9 of Law No. 26 of 1975 bars a person naturalised under
  these provisions from exercising political rights for **five years** from the date of acquisition,
  and from being elected or appointed to any parliamentary body for **ten years**. The President may
  grant exemptions by decree.
- **The citizenship can be withdrawn.** The Cabinet's own FAQ confirms withdrawal within **ten years**
  where it was obtained by fraud or false statements, and within **five years** on conviction for a
  felony or an honour crime (Law 26/1975, Articles 15–16, by reasoned Cabinet decree).
- **Dual nationality is permitted** by Egypt, and there is **no Arabic language test**.
- **Naturalised children are exempt from military service.**
