overview: |
  Vanuatu grants citizenship for a contribution under the Citizenship Act [Cap 112], through the
  Development Support Programme and the Vanuatu Contribution Programme, with a Capital Investment
  Immigration Plan and a Real Estate Option Programme alongside them. Citizenship is lifetime and
  inheritable, dual citizenship is permitted, and there is no residence requirement. Travel access has
  changed fundamentally: Regulation (EU) 2025/11 permanently revoked the EU visa waiver with effect from
  3 February 2025 — the only time this has happened to any country — and the United Kingdom removed
  visa-free access on 19 July 2023. Both decisions cite this citizenship programme. The passport now
  reaches roughly 88 destinations.

faq:
  - q: "How long does the process take?"
    a: "Vanuatu publishes no official processing standard. The only official duration is a three-month window after approval to complete the oath, extendable by two months."
  - q: "Can family members be included?"
    a: "For the Capital Investment Immigration Plan the official schedule confirms spouse, children under 18, and dependants aged 18 to 21 at a higher fee. For the contribution routes the dependant definition is set by Order No. 33 of 2019; XIPHIAS confirms the age limits that apply to your family on each file."
  - q: "What is the minimum investment?"
    a: "USD 130,000 for a single applicant, USD 150,000 for a married couple, USD 165,000 for a couple and one child, USD 180,000 for a couple and two children, and USD 10,000 for each additional applicant, plus a USD 5,000 Financial Intelligence Unit due diligence fee."
  - q: "Can I travel to Europe or the UK visa-free?"
    a: "No. Regulation (EU) 2025/11 moved Vanuatu into Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 with effect from 3 February 2025, permanently revoking the visa waiver — the only time the EU has done this to any country. The United Kingdom imposed a visa requirement on 19 July 2023. Both cite this citizenship programme."
  - q: "Are there taxes on citizenship?"
    a: "Vanuatu levies no personal or corporate income tax, confirmed by the IMF, and VAT is 15%. But Vanuatu is currently on the EU list of non-cooperative jurisdictions for tax purposes and on the EU list of high-risk third countries for anti-money-laundering deficiencies. Banks act on both, so the tax position should not be read in isolation."
  - q: "Is residency required?"
    a: "No, not for the investment routes. An oath is required; the official sources conflict on whether it must be taken in Vanuatu or can be done by video, so XIPHIAS does not promise a remote oath."
