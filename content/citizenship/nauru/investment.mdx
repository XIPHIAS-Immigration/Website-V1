## Overview

Nauru's **Economic and Climate Resilience Citizenship Programme**, established by the Economic and
Climate Resilience Citizenship Act 2024, grants citizenship for a single contribution. It is a
contribution route only — there is no property option — and the published decision standard is three
to four months.

Two recent changes matter more than the price: the **United Kingdom withdrew visa-free access in
December 2025**, and the **fee schedule was cut and restructured**.

## The United Kingdom removed visa-free access — because of this programme

On **9 December 2025 at 15:00 GMT** the United Kingdom imposed a visa requirement on Nauru nationals,
by Statement of Changes **HC 1491**, announced the same day in Written Ministerial Statement
**HCWS1143**. The stated reason was **this exact programme**: the UK's position is that granting
citizenship through investment is inherently high-risk and creates vulnerability to criminal
exploitation and circumvention of immigration controls.

Nauru nationals also **lost ETA eligibility** and now need a standard visit visa or a Direct Airside
Transit Visa. The grace period for existing ETA holders expired on 20 January 2026. Nauru sits at
item 74 on the UK's visa national list today.

A Nauru passport no longer gives visa-free access to the United Kingdom.

## The EU and Schengen: the listing is dormant, not live

Nauru **is** in Annex II of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806, the visa-exempt list, but with a footnote: the
exemption applies only **from the date an EU–Nauru visa waiver agreement enters into force**. No such
agreement exists. The Council's own material says so, and Germany's Federal Foreign Office lists
Nauru as visa-required in practice.

So there is **no Schengen access** on a Nauru passport.

For context on where this is heading: Vanuatu was moved out of Annex II entirely, with effect from
3 February 2025, over its own investment-citizenship scheme.

## Before anything else: what this means for an Indian passport

**India does not allow dual citizenship.** Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955 says that any
citizen of India who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country **shall cease to be a
citizen of India**.

Acquiring Nauruan citizenship is voluntary. So your Indian citizenship ends **automatically, by operation
of law, on the day you acquire it**. There is no Indian decision to appeal, no discretion to exercise
and no way to defer it. You must surrender your Indian passport and obtain a Surrender Certificate.

You can then apply for **Overseas Citizen of India** status. The Ministry of External Affairs is
explicit that *OCI is not to be misconstrued as dual citizenship* and confers no political rights. As
an OCI you cannot vote, cannot hold constitutional posts, do not have equality in public employment,
and **cannot acquire agricultural land, farmhouses or plantation property in India**.

This is a **replacement** of your Indian citizenship, not an addition to it. Nauru itself permits
dual citizenship and requires no renunciation — the restriction is entirely on the Indian side.

The USD 90,000 reflects a **USD 25,000 reduction applying to applications filed before 31 December
2026**. The programme publishes only the reduced figure and the size of the reduction; it does not
publish a standard undiscounted price.

There are **no family-size tiers**: no "family of 2–4" or "family of 5+" price. A single applicant
today pays roughly **USD 102,700 all in**.

**There is no real estate option.** The Act provides a contribution route only.

## Who can be included, and on what conditions

The Act sets material conditions on each category.

- **Spouse** — monogamous marriage or de facto relationship.
- **Children under 18** — biological or adopted, included without further condition.
- **Children aged 18–30** — only if **unmarried and fully supported** by the principal applicant.
- **Adult children over 18** — if physically or mentally challenged and living with and fully
  supported by the principal applicant.
- **Parents and grandparents over 55** — only if **fully supported** by the principal applicant.
- **Siblings** — only if **not married and never married**. No age limit is stated.

## What the passport actually reaches

The programme's own FAQ says **"over 85 countries"** and names only **Hong Kong SAR (90 days),
Singapore and the UAE**. That is the government's own figure.

Independent indices put it at around 86 destinations for 2026 and explicitly record the UK as
visa-required since 9 December 2025.

Hong Kong and Singapore access is confirmed in those governments' own published lists. The UAE is
reported as 90 days visa-free; XIPHIAS confirms the current UAE entry position with the UAE
authorities for each file.

## Process, timing and the interview

The published standard is **three to four months** from submission to a citizenship decision.

There is **no residence or physical presence requirement** — no minimum stay, no visit, and the
process can be completed without travelling to Nauru.

But **an interview is mandatory for every principal applicant**, and dependants aged 16 and over may
also be required to attend. It may be held virtually or by audio-visual link, so it does not force a
trip, but it is a firm requirement.

## What this citizenship is, in law

The Act creates **"economic and climate resilience citizenship"** as a **distinct statutory category**,
separate from citizenship under the Naoero Citizenship Act 2017. The Act is silent on whether it
carries the right to reside in Nauru, to vote, to own land or to hold office — there is no express
restriction, but equally no express grant. Clients should not assume it confers a right to live in
Nauru.

Cancellation grounds do exist: fraud, terrorist financing, becoming a designated person, or becoming
a citizen of a UN-listed high-risk country. Ineligibility covers terrorism, money laundering,
organised crime and child sexual offences.

**Nauru does permit dual citizenship** — section 16 of the Naoero Citizenship Act 2017 is explicit,
and renunciation is voluntary. The restriction that matters for an Indian client is entirely on the
Indian side.
