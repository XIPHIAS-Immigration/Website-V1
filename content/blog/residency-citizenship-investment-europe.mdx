## Investment Options in Europe (3 Categories)

1. **Property / Real Estate Investment**
2. **Business Investment**
3. **Investment in Government Bonds / Public Debt**

Countries like **Greece** offer their **residency** to investors who invest in the country’s **real estate**: **EUR 800,000** in Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Thira (Santorini) and the larger islands, **EUR 400,000** elsewhere, and **EUR 250,000** for converting commercial buildings or restoring listed buildings. Most of these programs are developed with an intention to encourage direct investments into the **real estate** and development in the country. One of the most famous investment programs is the **Portugal Golden Visa program**, but Portugal removed real estate from it in October 2023; its main route today is **EUR 500,000** in qualifying investment funds. **Malta** and **Cyprus** offer **residency** to **high net-worth investors** through real estate and other qualifying investments. Their citizenship by investment routes have closed: Cyprus ended its programme on 1 November 2020, and Malta's was repealed in 2025.

**Business Investments** can be categorized into two. One of the categories requires the investor to be a **passive investor** while the other investment program requires the investor to be an **active investor** where the investor will be part of the everyday activity of running the business. **UK**, **France**, **Germany** and few other countries provide **Investment programs** which encourage innovative **entrepreneurs** to set up their **business** in the country. These countries provide **residency** to the investor which eventually can be converted as would be required. **French Talent Passport Program** is one of these programs which provide the investor best **investment immigration** opportunities to explore the beautiful and prosperous country. These investments will need the investor to prove his or her caliber and experience as a **businessman** for a continuous period of time.

**Government bonds** are another type of investment that some **European countries** have accepted. At the end of the said period, the investor can get back his investment. Many bond routes have now closed: the **UK** closed its Tier 1 Investor visa, which required **£2 million**, in February 2022, and **Latvia** removed its government securities route. Today the **UK** offers residence to entrepreneurs through the **Innovator Founder visa**, which can lead to **permanent living** in the country.

**Investors** depending on their goal should choose the **right investment program**. Usually the **investors** choose an investment program depending on whether he would get **residency** in the continent or **citizenship** of a country there.

## Residency in Europe through Investments

This is by far the most sought after investment type. Usually the **investor** first achieves **temporary residence** in the continent followed by **permanent residency** in the country. Most of the **residency programs** require the **investor** to maintain their **investment** in the country for a stipulated period of time. However, rules for those countries offering **permanent residency** and **temporary residence** are different. Most of the programs over period of time ensure the **immigrant** obtains **citizenship** of the country where he or she is a **resident** of. The **tax regimes** are favorable for the **investors**. The **residency** is usually extended to the **immediate family** of the **investor**. **Malta** provides **permanent residency** to the **investor** through the **Malta Permanent Residence Programme**. Few famous countries which offer **residency of Europe** are **Bulgaria**, **Cyprus**, **Greece**, **Hungary**, **Latvia** (through business investment), **Malta** and **Portugal**. **Spain** ended its Golden Visa on 3 April 2025.

## Europe’s Citizenship by Investment Program

Through such programmes, a **high net-worth individual** earned direct **citizenship** in return for an investment, usually including a **non-refundable contribution** to **Government funds**. These programmes were much more **expensive** than **residency programs**, and within the EU they have now closed: **Cyprus** ended its programme on 1 November 2020, **Bulgaria** repealed its route on 1 April 2022, and **Malta's** programme was repealed in 2025 after the Court of Justice of the EU ruled against it on 29 April 2025. In Europe today, citizenship comes through naturalisation after years of residence.

## FAQs

**Which European countries are popular for Residency by Investment?**
**Portugal**, **Greece**, **Malta** and **Hungary** are widely known for **residency by investment** via **real estate**, **investment funds**, or **business** routes.

**What’s the difference between Residency by Investment and Citizenship by Investment?**
**Residency by Investment** grants the right to **live and work** in the country (often leading to **PR**), while **Citizenship by Investment** provides **second citizenship** and **passport**—usually requiring a **non-refundable contribution** or **larger investment**.

**Is real estate the only way to qualify?**
No. Besides **real estate**, options often include **business investment** (active/passive) and **investment funds**—depending on the **country’s program**. In Portugal, real estate no longer qualifies at all.

**Can my family be included in the application?**
Yes. Most **residency** and **citizenship** routes allow inclusion of **spouse** and **dependent children**, and some include **parents**—subject to the specific **program rules**.
