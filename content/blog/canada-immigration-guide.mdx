Canada is one of the most versatile countries currently inviting skilled professionals and entrepreneurs to relocate. Under its 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan, Canada plans to welcome around 380,000 new permanent residents each year. With over **70 immigration programs**, Canada provides multiple pathways to permanent residency, including the **Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)**.

## Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)

Started in the 1990s, the PNP allows provinces to nominate immigrants based on local labor needs. Each province has its own program to address specific immigration requirements. With nomination in hand, candidates can apply for **Permanent Residency in Canada**.

Many PNP pathways are linked with the **Express Entry System**, a points-based profile ranking system that evaluates age, education, experience, adaptability, and language proficiency.

## Skilled Professionals

Each province selects the right candidates to meet its labor demands:

- **Ontario**: Technical hub and political center, which nominates skilled workers through the **Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP)**.  
- **British Columbia (BC)**: Active in technical and skilled migration programs.  
- **Saskatchewan**: Offers excellent living conditions and growing job opportunities.

## Entrepreneurs & Business Immigrants

Entrepreneurs can establish startups, invest, or acquire businesses in Canada. Key programs include:

- **New Brunswick Business Immigration Stream**: requires a minimum investment of **CAD 150,000** and a net worth of **CAD 500,000** (**CAD 300,000** for a farming business).  
- Provinces like **British Columbia, Alberta, and Manitoba** also actively invite investors, including farm investors. Ontario’s entrepreneur stream has closed, and the federal Start-Up Visa stopped accepting new applications on 31 December 2025.

A robust business plan is required to demonstrate intent, expertise, and contribution to the province.

## International Students

Students can apply to Canadian universities and may obtain scholarships. Graduates can transition into the workforce easily, contributing to the country’s economic growth.

## Working with CICC-Licensed Consultants

To ensure smooth and secure relocation, it is recommended to work with consultants licensed by the **College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC)**, which replaced the ICCRC in November 2021. They provide expert guidance on Canadian immigration programs and help applicants meet requirements efficiently.
