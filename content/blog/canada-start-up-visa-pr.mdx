## The Start-Up Visa is closed to new applicants

Canada stopped accepting new **Start-Up Visa (SUV)** applications on **31 December 2025**. Holders of a valid 2025 commitment certificate had until **30 June 2026** to file, and IRCC paused the programme on that date. The optional SUV work permit stopped being accepted on **19 December 2025**, except extensions for people already in Canada on one. IRCC continues to process applications it accepted before the cut-off.

IRCC has said a new, targeted pilot programme for immigrant entrepreneurs will follow; as at 29 September 2026, none had launched on canada.ca. The 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan sets federal business admissions at **500 a year**, so for entrepreneurs planning a move now, the open routes are the provincial business streams listed further down.

The Start-Up Visa began on **1 April 2013** to attract founders who could **create jobs** in **Canada** through new ventures. The sections below describe how it worked, for anyone with an application still in process.

## How the Programme Worked

Applicants needed **support** from a **designated organization** in **Canada**:

- **Venture Capital Fund** (designated)
- **Angel Investor Group** (designated)
- **Business Incubator** (designated)

Each organization ran its **own process** (intake forms, pitch deck, interviews) and, if it decided to back the business, issued a **Letter of Support** (commitment certificate). No application could be filed without one.

## What the SUV Required (for Reference)

### 1) Designated Support & Investment
- **VC fund**: minimum **CAD 200,000** investment **or**
- **Angel group**: minimum **CAD 75,000** investment **or**
- **Business incubator**: **acceptance** into the program (no minimum cash required)

### 2) Language Requirement
- Applicants needed **Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 5** in **English or French** across **reading, writing, listening, and speaking** (via an approved test such as **IELTS/CELPIP** or **TEF/TCF**).

### 3) Ownership & Control
- Up to **five** people could apply as **owners** of one business.
- Each **applicant** had to hold **at least 10%** of voting rights.
- The **applicants + designated organization(s)** had to **jointly** hold **more than 50%** of voting rights.
- Founders had to play an **active, day-to-day** role in the venture.

### 4) Settlement Funds
- Applicants had to show **sufficient settlement funds** for themselves and their family, since the government did **not** provide financial support to **SUV** applicants. The amount depended on **family size**.

## Routes into Canada That Are Still Open

The federal business routes have closed, but several provinces and territories still run business streams that lead to permanent residence:

- [British Columbia — Entrepreneur Immigration, Base stream](/residency/canada/british-columbia-entrepreneur-base) — from CAD 200,000
- [British Columbia — Entrepreneur Immigration, Regional stream](/residency/canada/british-columbia-regional-pilot) — from CAD 100,000
- [Prince Edward Island — Work Permit Stream](/residency/canada/pei-work-permit) — from CAD 150,000
- [Newfoundland & Labrador — International Entrepreneur](/residency/canada/newfoundland-international-entrepreneur) — from CAD 200,000
- [Nova Scotia — Entrepreneur stream](/residency/canada/nova-scotia-entrepreneur-stream) — from CAD 100,000
- [Northwest Territories — Business Stream](/residency/canada/northwest-territories-business) — from CAD 100,000
- [New Brunswick — Business Immigration Stream](/residency/canada/new-brunswick-business-immigration) — from CAD 150,000
- [Manitoba — Business Investor Stream, Entrepreneur Pathway](/residency/canada/manitoba-entrepreneur)
- [Alberta — Self-Employed Farmer Stream](/residency/canada/alberta-self-employed-farmer)
- [Yukon — Business Nominee Program](/residency/canada/yukon-business) — from CAD 300,000

Unlike the SUV, these streams usually require you to run the business in the province for a period, under a performance agreement, before you are nominated for permanent residence. The federal Self-Employed Persons Program is also paused, Ontario has closed its Entrepreneur Stream, and Saskatchewan permanently closed its entrepreneur and farm pathways on 27 March 2025.

**FAQs**

**1) Can I still apply for the Canada Start-Up Visa?**
No. New applications stopped on 31 December 2025, and the last window, for holders of a valid 2025 commitment certificate, closed on 30 June 2026.

**2) What happens to an application I have already filed?**
IRCC continues to process applications it accepted before the cut-off. The closure does not cancel a file already in the system.

**3) Is there a replacement programme?**
IRCC has said a targeted pilot programme for immigrant entrepreneurs will follow. As at 29 September 2026, none had launched on canada.ca.

**4) Can I still get a Start-Up Visa work permit?**
No new SUV work permits have been accepted since 19 December 2025, except extensions for people already in Canada on one.

**5) What should an entrepreneur consider instead?**
The provincial business streams listed above, and, for skilled profiles, Express Entry and the Provincial Nominee Program.
