**Cross-border investments** are interesting propositions. Every high-net-worth businessman who likes challenges and loves to expand self and their business prospects invests in a foreign country, which is also a welcome challenge for them. Many countries invite foreign direct investments, and these programs are designed to help them fund projects—whether **government-based**, **research**, or **business investments**.

Of all these categories, it is **Business Investment Immigration** that attracts the most number of high-net-worth individuals. The thrill of doing business in a foreign land and the returns these investments can generate are two things a businessman can never forego. Every country has its own dedicated immigration program, defining their requirements and importance for such investments.


## 1. Business Investment Programs

From **Canada** to **Australia**, many countries have business houses that contribute to economic development and generate employment opportunities. However, some businesses need external funding, which is where investment programs like the **Canada Owner Operator Program** and the **UK Innovator Founder visa** come in.

- **Canada**'s provincial entrepreneur streams issue work permits to business investors and nominate them for permanent residency once the business meets the conditions of a performance agreement.
- The **UK Innovator Founder visa** allows investors to benefit from the best infrastructure and support to set up their businesses.
- **Europe** also offers **permanent residency** to investors looking to invest in businesses.


## 2. Farm Investments

Some Canadian provinces run farm streams under their **Provincial Nominee Programs**, such as Alberta's Self-Employed Farmer Stream, for applicants who can prove their experience in farming. Saskatchewan permanently closed its Farm Owner/Operator pathway on 27 March 2025, and **Australia** no longer runs a business or investor migration program: its Business Innovation and Investment Program closed on 31 July 2024.


## 3. Influx of Innovative Ideas and Growth of Economy

**Canada** encouraged innovators through its **Start-up Visa program**, which stopped accepting new applications on 31 December 2025 and was paused on 30 June 2026; entrepreneurs now use provincial business streams instead. The **UK** supports businesses created from new, innovative ideas to provide **employment** and allow the business to scale internationally.


## 4. Real Estate Boom

Countries in **Europe** and the **Caribbean Islands** invite foreign investments in **real estate**. For example, **Greece's Golden Visa** requires a property investment of €400,000, or €800,000 in Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Thira and larger islands, and countries like **Cyprus**, **Monaco** and **Malta** also encourage property investment for obtaining residency. **Portugal**, by contrast, no longer accepts real estate for its Golden Visa. The **Caribbean** countries offer **citizenship by investment** to those who invest in real estate, providing the best second passport programs.

Many of these countries have beautiful beaches and booming tourism sectors, providing excellent returns on investment in **real estate**.


## 5. Funds for Research and Development Programs

Apart from real estate and farm investments, several countries invite investments in **research and development**. **Portugal's Golden Visa** accepts capital transfers to approved research institutions within the national scientific system, and **Hungary's** Guest Investor Programme accepts a donation of at least **€1,000,000** to a higher education institution for education, scientific research or artistic purposes.


## 6. Funds for Government Projects

Countries like **Vanuatu** require **foreign direct investment** to support their development. The **Vanuatu CIB** program offers citizenship to investors willing to contribute to the country’s development. Vanuatu nationals have needed a UK visa since 19 July 2023, and the EU permanently revoked Vanuatu's visa waiver with effect from 3 February 2025. Several **Caribbean countries** also offer **citizenship by investment** (CIB) programs, such as **Grenada’s CIB**; Grenada is a US E-2 treaty country (in force since 3 March 1989), and E-2 eligibility for an individual investor is decided by US consular officers.

European countries also encourage investment to fund **government projects** while offering **permanent residency**.


## 7. Development Projects

Countries benefiting from foreign investments often direct those funds into **development projects** like public utilities and business developments. These projects help improve the living conditions of the citizens and offer **second passports** with the potential for visa-free travel to more than 100 countries.

Investors who approach countries with these opportunities through **registered immigration agents** can benefit from such programs. Many **Caribbean island countries** specifically require investors to work through registered agents.

## FAQs

**1. What is the importance of investment programs?**
- Investment programs allow individuals to contribute to the development of foreign countries while gaining residency or citizenship in return.

**2. Which countries offer investment programs?**
- Countries like **Canada** (through provincial streams), **Portugal**, **Greece**, **Hungary**, **the UK**, and **Caribbean islands** offer investment or business immigration programs.

**3. What are the benefits of real estate investment?**
- **Greece** provides residency through real estate investment, and Caribbean countries such as **Grenada** and **Dominica** offer citizenship through approved real estate. **Portugal** no longer accepts real estate for its Golden Visa.

**4. How can I invest in farm land in Canada?**
- Provincial farm streams, such as Alberta's Self-Employed Farmer Stream, allow experienced farmers to buy and run a farm and work toward permanent residency. Australia has no open business or investor migration route.

**5. Can I still apply for the Canada Start-up Visa?**
- No. New applications stopped on 31 December 2025 and the programme was paused on 30 June 2026. Entrepreneurs now apply through provincial business streams.
