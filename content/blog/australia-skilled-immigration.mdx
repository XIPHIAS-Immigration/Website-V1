## Introduction

The introduction of the **Skilled Visa Australia** has opened opportunities for individuals to **migrate and contribute to Australia’s growth**.  
The [Skilled Worker Visa in Australia](https://www.xiphiasimmigration.com/skilled/australia) caters to specialists and skilled professionals, including those sponsored by employers or approved under a **State Migration Plan**, offering additional benefits.

Australia's skilled migration runs through **three main routes**, each with its own purpose and requirements. Understanding them ensures applicants meet eligibility criteria.

---

## Main Skilled Migration Programs

### 1. Points-Tested Skilled Migration
- Candidates are assessed on a **points test** and must be **invited** through SkillSelect
- 65 points is the minimum, but recent rounds have needed far more: in the 4 June 2026 round, professional occupations were invited at **80 to 100 points**, and no invitations had been issued in the 2026–27 programme year as of September 2026

### 2. Employer Sponsored Migration
- Sponsored by an **Australian employer**, with **no points test**
- Includes temporary visas that lead to permanent residence, and direct permanent visas

### 3. National Innovation Visa (Subclass 858)
- The former Global Talent visa, renamed and restructured on **6 December 2024**
- For people with an internationally recognised record of exceptional achievement; a **nominator** with a national reputation in the applicant's field and a **government invitation** are both mandatory

Australia no longer runs a business or investor migration program: the Business Innovation and Investment Program (Subclasses 188 and 888) closed permanently on **31 July 2024**.

## Visa Types to Immigrate to Australia

### 1. Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189)
- Points-based system  
- No sponsorship needed  
- Work and live in Australia as **permanent residents**

### 2. Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190)
- Points-based, with **nomination by a state or territory government** (worth 5 points)  
- Work and live as permanent residents

### 3. Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa (Subclass 491)
- Live and work in **designated regional areas**  
- A **5-year** visa, with permanent residence through **Subclass 191** after 3 years  
- Requires **state or territory nomination or sponsorship by an eligible family member**, and an **invitation to apply**

### 4. Skills in Demand Visa (Subclass 482)
- Australia's main employer-sponsored work visa, for **up to 4 years**  
- Requires **employer sponsorship**; only **1 year** of relevant work experience is needed  
- Replaced the Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa on **7 December 2024**; the former Subclass 457 visa no longer exists

### 5. Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186)
- **Permanent visa** for skilled workers  
- Three streams: **Temporary Residence Transition**, **Direct Entry**, and **Labour Agreement**

### 6. Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional Visa (Subclass 494)
- Sponsored by an **employer in regional Australia** for a **5-year** visa  
- Permanent residence through **Subclass 191** after 3 years  
- Replaced the Regional Sponsored Migration Scheme (Subclass 187), which closed to new applications on **16 November 2019**

## Eligibility Criteria

To qualify for a points-tested skilled visa (Subclasses 189, 190 and 491), applicants must:

- Receive an **invitation from the Australian Government**
- Have an occupation listed on the relevant **skilled occupation list**
- Achieve the required **points score**
- Demonstrate **English language proficiency**
- Be **under 45 years of age** at the time of invitation

Employer-sponsored visas have no points test and set their own age and experience rules.

## FAQs

**1. What are the main Skilled Visa programs in Australia?**  
Subclass 189 (Skilled Independent), Subclass 190 (Skilled Nominated), Subclass 491 (Skilled Work Regional), Subclass 482 (Skills in Demand), Subclass 186 (Employer Nomination Scheme), Subclass 494 (Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional) and Subclass 858 (National Innovation). Subclasses 187, 457 and 489 are closed.

**2. Who is eligible for Skilled Migration?**  
For the points-tested visas, skilled professionals under 45 at invitation who meet the points, English proficiency, and occupation criteria. Employer-sponsored visas have no points test.

**3. Can employer sponsorship increase chances of approval?**  
Yes. Employer-sponsored visas skip the points test entirely, and state nomination or sponsorship by an eligible family member adds points for the 190 and 491 visas.

**4. How long does it take to get permanent residency?**  
It depends on the visa type. Some visas offer **immediate permanent residency**, while others require a **temporary period first**.
