**The Canada Start-up Visa is closed to new applicants.** Canada stopped accepting new applications on 31 December 2025; holders of a valid 2025 commitment certificate had until 30 June 2026 to file, and IRCC paused the programme on that date. IRCC continues to process applications it accepted before the cut-off. If you are an entrepreneur planning a move to Canada now, the open routes are the provincial business streams listed later in this article.

The Canada Start-up Visa was designed to attract talented entrepreneurs worldwide—including India—to start innovative and competitive businesses in Canada. Unlike traditional immigration programs, it worked through a partnership model connecting entrepreneurs with Canadian investors, accelerators, and incubators.

## What the Start-up Visa Offered

1. Strong startup ecosystem: Canada hosts a vibrant community of startups, incubators, universities, and research institutions, and the Start-up Visa connected entrepreneurs directly to it.

2. Direct permanent residency: The program gave entrepreneurs and their families a path to Canada PR, with an optional work permit to start the business while the application was processed.

3. Open to entrepreneurs worldwide: Canada is known for its welcoming and inclusive environment, and the Start-up Visa was open to entrepreneurs from any country, including India.

4. Global market access: Canada’s location and trade relationships provide entrepreneurs easy access to markets in North America, Europe, and Asia.

5. Multiple applicants for one business: Up to five people could apply as owners of a single business if they met program criteria.

6. No age or higher education requirements: There was no age restriction, minimum net worth, or higher education requirement.

## What It Required (for Reference)

Entrepreneurs had to propose a scalable and innovative start-up business approved by a designated organization.

Options were:
- Business incubation: no minimum investment required
- Venture capital fund: raise CAD 200,000 from a designated fund
- Angel investment: raise CAD 75,000 from a designated angel group

Additional requirements:
- English or French language proficiency at CLB 5
- No criminal record
- Good health

## What Is Open Now

The federal business routes have closed, and the 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan sets federal business admissions at 500 a year. Provincial business streams still accepting applications include [British Columbia](/residency/canada/british-columbia-entrepreneur-base), [Prince Edward Island](/residency/canada/pei-work-permit), [Newfoundland & Labrador](/residency/canada/newfoundland-international-entrepreneur), [Nova Scotia](/residency/canada/nova-scotia-entrepreneur-stream), [Northwest Territories](/residency/canada/northwest-territories-business), [New Brunswick](/residency/canada/new-brunswick-business-immigration), [Manitoba](/residency/canada/manitoba-entrepreneur), [Alberta](/residency/canada/alberta-self-employed-farmer) and [Yukon](/residency/canada/yukon-business). These usually require you to run a business in the province under a performance agreement before you are nominated for permanent residence.

## FAQs

1. Can I still apply for the Canada Start-up Visa?
- No. New applications stopped on 31 December 2025, and the final window for holders of a valid 2025 commitment certificate closed on 30 June 2026.

2. What happens to applications already filed?
- IRCC continues to process applications it accepted before the cut-off.

3. Is there a replacement programme?
- IRCC has said a targeted pilot programme for immigrant entrepreneurs will follow. As at 29 September 2026, none had launched.

4. How are Canada PR points calculated?
- CRS points: Age (up to 110), Education (up to 150), Language proficiency (up to 136), Work experience (up to 80), Spouse/partner factors (up to 40), Additional factors such as a provincial nomination. Maximum CRS score: 1,200.

5. How did the Start-up Visa differ from Express Entry?
- Both led to permanent residency, but the Start-up Visa depended on support from a designated organization rather than a CRS score. Express Entry remains open; the Start-up Visa is closed.
