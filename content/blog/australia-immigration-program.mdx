## Introduction

Australia, a prominent island country in the southern hemisphere, is known for its **economic strength**, **high living standards**, and **cultural diversity**. Australia runs programs designed to attract skilled professionals, and many visa streams provide **Permanent Residency (PR)**, allowing migrants to live, work, study, and invest in the country.

## SkillSelect and Expression of Interest (EOI)

Australia’s **SkillSelect portal** is the first step for most skilled migrants. Applicants submit an **Expression of Interest (EOI)** with supporting documents.

- Profiles remain in the **SkillSelect pool for 2 years**.
- Points are allocated based on **age, education, work experience, adaptability, and language proficiency**.
- EOIs are **not visa applications**; they express interest in migrating.

Selection is based on **points and the current occupation list** in Australia. Only shortlisted candidates receive invitations to apply for a visa.

## Key Visa Programs

#### Sub Class 189: Skilled Independent Visa
- For skilled professionals **without a sponsor or nominator**.
- Applicants must be **under 45 years of age**.
- Points-tested program based on **age, education, work experience, adaptability, and language**. 65 points is the minimum, but most professional occupations needed 80 to 100 points in 2026.
- Allows permanent residency, work, study, and freedom to choose employers.
- **Processing time:** about 12 months (XIPHIAS average).

#### Sub Class 190: Skilled Nominated Visa
- For professionals **nominated by a state or territory government**.
- Occupation must be on the state or territory’s skilled occupation list.
- Points-tested program; state nomination adds 5 points.
- Permanent residency granted; eligibility to apply for **citizenship after 4 years**.
- Applicants are expected to live and work in the nominating state or territory for an initial period.

#### Sub Class 491: Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) Visa
- For professionals nominated by **state, territory, or eligible relative**.
- Valid for **5 years**, with the right to live, work and study in designated regional areas.
- After 3 years holding the visa and meeting the income requirement, holders can apply for permanent residency through **Subclass 191**.


## Business and Investor Pathways
Australia’s Business Innovation and Investment Program (Subclasses 188 and 888) closed permanently to new applicants on **31 July 2024**, and there is currently no open federal business or investor visa.
- Founders and investors with an exceptional record may qualify for the **National Innovation visa (Subclass 858)**.
- Employer-sponsored routes such as the **Skills in Demand visa (Subclass 482)** and the **Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186)** remain open.

## Living in Australia
- **Primary education** is free for residents and PR holders.
- **Healthcare system** is well-managed and cost-effective.
- High **living standards** and well-maintained neighborhoods.
- PR holders can sponsor **family members** under selected streams.

## FAQs

**1. What is the Sub Class 189 visa?**
A points-based visa for skilled professionals without sponsors, granting PR with freedom to work and study anywhere in Australia.

**2. Who is eligible for the Sub Class 190 visa?**
Professionals nominated by a state or territory government to fill in-demand occupations.

**3. What is the Sub Class 491 visa?**
A provisional regional visa for nominated skilled professionals, leading to permanent residency and citizenship eligibility.

**4. Can business investors apply for PR in Australia?**
Not through a dedicated investor visa: the **Business Innovation and Investment Program** closed to new applicants on 31 July 2024. Exceptional founders and investors may qualify for the **National Innovation visa (Subclass 858)**.

**5. Can PR holders sponsor family members?**
Yes, under select immigration streams, PR holders can invite spouses, partners, and dependent children.
