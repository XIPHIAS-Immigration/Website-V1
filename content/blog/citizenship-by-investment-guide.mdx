## Meaning of Citizenship

A **citizen** is a person legally recognized by a country, with rights such as **voting**, **working**, and **owning property**, and duties to **respect the nation’s laws**. Traditionally, citizenship is obtained **by birth**, **by naturalization** (after residence), or **through marriage**.

In the 1980s, a new pathway emerged: **Citizenship by Investment (CBI)**, also called **economic citizenship**.

## What Is Citizenship by Investment (CBI)?

Certain countries offer **citizenship** to **high-net-worth** applicants who make a **qualifying investment**. In return, investors often obtain a **second passport**, enhanced global mobility, and access to new markets. **International investments** via CBI have drawn growing interest worldwide.

## Why Countries Offer CBI (Government Perspective)

- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI):** Funds **public** and **private** development (e.g., **Vanuatu** channels donations into national projects).
- **Business & Jobs:** Encourages entrepreneurs to **set up companies** and **create employment**.
- **Population Strategy:** Some countries use CBI to **attract new residents** and talent.

## Investor Benefits

- **Second passport & mobility:** Visa-free and visa-on-arrival access varies widely by passport and can change. The UK has required visas from Dominica and Vanuatu nationals since 19 July 2023 and from Saint Lucia nationals since 5 March 2026, and the EU permanently revoked Vanuatu's visa waiver with effect from 3 February 2025.
- **Global opportunity:** Expand markets and operations (e.g., **Caribbean CBI** opens regional and global gateways).
- **Tax:** Citizenship by itself does not change where you are tax resident; any tax benefit depends on actually becoming resident in the new country under its rules.
- **Dual citizenship:** Most CBI countries **allow dual nationality**, but India does not. Under Section 9(1) of the Citizenship Act 1955, an Indian citizen who voluntarily acquires another citizenship ceases to be an Indian citizen automatically; OCI is available afterwards but is not dual citizenship.
- **Family inclusion:** Spouse and **dependent children** (and sometimes parents) can be added. **Dominica’s** framework even supports **larger families** under set conditions.
- **Education & lifestyle:** Island programs offer high-quality living environments. Within the EU, **Malta**, **Cyprus** and **Bulgaria** have all closed their citizenship-by-investment schemes, so investment now leads to EU residence rather than EU citizenship.

> **Note:** Benefits, destinations, and tax rules vary by country—always review the latest official program criteria.

## Typical Eligibility & Documents

- **Age:** Usually **18+** for the main applicant.
- **Clean background:** **Police clearance** for all adults included.
- **Medical fitness:** **Health certificates** confirming good health.
- **Source of funds:** Documented, **lawful** origin of investment funds.
- **Program compliance:** Invest via **approved routes** and meet due-diligence requirements.

## Main Investment Routes

1. **Real Estate:** Pre-approved **hotels/resorts/residential** projects with a **minimum hold period**.
2. **Business/Enterprise:** Invest as a **passive** or **active** partner, sometimes with **job-creation** targets.
3. **National Funds/Donations:** Non-refundable contributions to government funds or **sovereign projects**.

## Notable Mentions from This Guide

- **Vanuatu — Citizenship by Investment:** A contribution-based programme supporting national development. Vanuatu nationals have needed a UK visa since 19 July 2023, and the EU permanently revoked Vanuatu's Schengen visa waiver with effect from 3 February 2025.
- **Dominica — CBI:** Family-friendly structures, from **USD 200,000** for a single applicant since 1 July 2024; celebrated as the **“Nature Isle.”** Dominica nationals need a UK visa, and US entry has been partially suspended under Proclamation 10998 since 1 January 2026.
- **Grenada — CBI:** From **USD 150,000** to the National Transformation Fund; the only Eastern Caribbean CBI country with a US E-2 treaty (in force since 3 March 1989).
- **Malta — Investment Migration:** Malta's citizenship-by-investment route was repealed in July 2025 after the Court of Justice of the EU ruled against it on 29 April 2025. The Malta Permanent Residence Programme, which grants residence rather than citizenship, remains open.

## FAQs

**Is donation or real estate better for CBI?**
Donations are simpler and faster but non-refundable. Real estate creates an asset with potential returns, but requires a hold period and exit planning.

**Do I or my family need to live in the country?**
Most CBI programs have no mandatory residence. Some require an oath, a brief visit or a short stay; Antigua and Barbuda, for example, requires 5 days in the country within the first five years.

**Who can I include in my application?**
Generally a spouse and dependent children; some programs allow dependent parents or grandparents. Eligibility and fees vary by country.

**How long does approval take?**
It depends on the program and the case. Dominica's Citizenship by Investment Unit, for example, states 3 to 4 months to approval in principle plus 3 to 6 weeks to naturalisation; St Kitts and Nevis states 120 to 180 days to approval in principle.
