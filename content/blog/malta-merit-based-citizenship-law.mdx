**Malta**, long known for its attractive **Citizenship by Investment** program, has officially scrapped the **Maltese Exceptional Investor Naturalization (MEIN)** policy. This bold move, prompted by legal pressure from the **European Union (EU)**, marks a significant shift in how **Malta** will offer **citizenship** to foreigners in the future.

So, what does this mean for **investors** and **global citizens**? Let’s break down what changed, what **citizenship by merit** is and is not, and why it matters in **2025**.

## What Was Malta's Citizenship by Investment Program?

The now-terminated **Malta Citizenship by Investment** program (officially known as **MEIN**) allowed **high-net-worth individuals** to acquire **Maltese citizenship** by making substantial **financial contributions** to the country. Successful applicants enjoyed **EU citizenship** benefits, such as unrestricted movement across the **EU** and **visa-free travel** to **180+ countries** worldwide.

But this program wasn’t without controversy. Critics, including **EU institutions**, argued that it commodified **EU citizenship** and undermined shared **European** values.

## Why Was It Terminated?

In **April 2025**, a significant **ECJ** ruling compelled **Malta** to discontinue its **Citizenship by Investment** law, citing violations of core **EU citizenship** principles. The court declared the **MEIN** policy inconsistent with **EU laws**, essentially stating that **EU citizenship** cannot be “bought.”

As a result, **Malta** repealed the scheme by **Act XXI of 2025**, published on 24 July 2025.

## Citizenship by Merit: What It Is and Is Not

Malta also has a separate, discretionary power to grant **citizenship by merit** to individuals who have made an **exceptional contribution** to the country. It rests on regulations dating from 2017, not on a new law.

Malta’s citizenship agency, Aġenzija Komunità Malta, states that citizenship by merit is **not a programme, scheme or pathway**, and is **not a continuation of, or an alternative to,** citizenship by investment. Decisions are discretionary and made case by case, and the agency does not permit the provisions to be marketed.

## How Does It Work?

- **No agents involved**: Unlike **MEIN**, there is no role for **licensed agents**.
- **Discretionary decisions**: Applications are assessed strictly, case by case, with no set price, investment or timeline.

This is not a route that can be bought or planned around, and **XIPHIAS Immigration** does not offer it as a service.

## What This Means for Indian Investors and Global Entrepreneurs

For **Indian investors** who previously looked at **Malta citizenship by investment** as a way into the **EU**, that route has closed, and citizenship by merit is not a substitute for it. The option open to investors is **residence** through the **[Malta Permanent Residence Programme (MPRP)](/residency/malta)**: a property purchase of at least **€375,000** or a lease of at least **€14,000 a year**, plus a **€60,000** administrative fee, a **€37,000** government contribution and a **€2,000** donation to a registered NGO.

Keep in mind that India does not allow dual citizenship: acquiring another country’s citizenship ends Indian citizenship.

## Final Thoughts

**Malta** has ended citizenship by investment. By repealing its **Citizenship by Investment (MEIN)** program after the Court of Justice ruling, **Malta** has aligned with **EU principles**: Maltese **citizenship** can no longer be bought. Investors who want a base in Malta should look at **residence** rather than citizenship.

## FAQs

**Does citizenship by merit allow fast citizenship like MEIN did?**
No. Citizenship by merit is **discretionary** and assessed case by case for **exceptional contributions**; it is not a programme and cannot be used as an investment route.

**Can entrepreneurs qualify for citizenship by merit?**
There is no business or investment threshold that qualifies anyone. Decisions are discretionary, and Malta does not permit the provisions to be marketed.

**Are agents allowed to file applications?**
No. Malta removed the role of agents when it repealed **MEIN**.

**What alternatives exist now that MEIN is ended?**
Consider **residency** options like the **[Malta Permanent Residence Program (MPRP)](/residency/malta)** for **EU mobility** without **citizenship**, or explore **other EU-compliant routes** aligned with your **profile**.
