# UK Visa Update 2025: Temporary Status, Longer ILR, and New Rules for Migrants

The UK government has announced plans for **big changes to immigration rules**. Shabana Mahmood, the Home Secretary, says that **living permanently in the UK is a privilege, not a right**.

---

## What the Government Has Proposed

- **Temporary Asylum:** Refugees would get protection for **2.5 years**, not permanent settlement. Their status would be reviewed, and if conditions improved at home, they could be sent back.  
- **Permanent Residency (ILR):** Indefinite Leave to Remain would take **10–20 years**, sometimes up to **30 years**, depending on work, contributions, and English proficiency.  
- **Welfare Access:** Migrants could access **benefits only after citizenship**, which would require living in the UK for at least 13 years.  
- **Overstayers & Deportation:** Illegal migrants, including “overstayers,” would face **faster deportation**, with only **one appeal** allowed, processed within weeks.

As at 1 October 2026, the settlement proposals have not been legislated. The earned-settlement consultation closed on 12 February 2026, and Statement of Changes HC 259 of 9 July 2026 made no change to settlement periods, so the current qualifying periods for ILR still apply.

---

## Why This Affects Indians

- Indians are the **sixth largest nationality seeking asylum** in the UK. Around **5,475 Indians applied for asylum** between July 2024 and June 2025. Most came on **legal visas** like student visas, then applied for asylum after their visa expired.  
- Indians also top the list of **overstayers**. Mahmood’s government plans to **identify and deport them faster**.  
- Countries that **refuse to take back deportees** could face **visa bans**. India may be affected if it does not cooperate.

---

## Impact on Legal Migrants

- The government proposes replacing the **automatic five-year path to permanent settlement** with a wait of **10 years or more**, with migrants required to demonstrate **job, income, contribution, and English skills**. Until the rules change, the current qualifying periods continue to apply.  
- Access to **social housing and government support** would be restricted until citizenship.  
- The proposals would also apply to migrants who arrived during the **“Boris wave”** from 2021 onwards.

---

## Tips for Indian Migrants

1. Keep all **visa and immigration documents updated**.  
2. **Follow UK rules carefully** to avoid delays or deportation.  
3. Plan for **long-term settlement**, knowing the waiting period may become longer than before.
